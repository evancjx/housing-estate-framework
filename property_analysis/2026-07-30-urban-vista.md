# Urban Vista — resale inventory, valuation and quantum analysis

Research captured: **2026-07-30 22:47:24 SGT (UTC+08:00)**  
Property: **Urban Vista, 2–20 Tanah Merah Kechil Link, Singapore**  
Analysis type: **property resale inventory, valuation and investment analysis**  
Status: **point-in-time market snapshot**

## Decision

Urban Vista has the lowest observed entry quantum among the immediate Tanah
Merah MRT resale cluster and healthy resale/rental activity. A conventional
441 sqft unit around S$730k–S$750k or a conventional 560–603 sqft unit around
S$900k–S$950k can be a defensible low-ticket purchase. The project also has
unusually wide price dispersion caused by floor, facing, loft, dual-key and
penthouse differences. Its low headline PSF is therefore **partly value and
partly product heterogeneity**, not a blanket market error.

The strongest very-low-quantum evidence is the **441 sqft cohort**: eight
trailing resales achieved S$722k–S$740k, with a S$729k median. The captured
S$748k ask is close enough to evidence to investigate; the S$830k “two-bedroom
loft” advertisement is about 12% above the latest registered 441 sqft caveat
and depends on approved loft utility, condition and a buyer willing to value
airspace that the bank may not.

The best balanced conventional investment cohort is **560–603 sqft**. Recent
resales include 560 sqft at S$915k and 592 sqft at S$903k. Captured asks of
S$938k–S$980k are plausible but range from modest to full premiums. A practical
entry target is **S$900k–S$940k for a normal 560–592 sqft apartment**, with
S$950k–S$980k reserved for a demonstrably superior 603–624 sqft unit.

For families, recent 786–797 sqft transactions near S$1.28m are more useful
than project averages. Advertised 850 sqft homes around S$1.45m–S$1.49m are
premium entries unless floor, view, renovation and exact-size caveats support
them. Penthouses at 1,055–1,249 sqft have low-looking PSF but should not be
compared with conventional single-level units.

At a representative **S$930k purchase of 592 sqft**, a simplified S$500,000
capital-only gain requires an exit around **S$1.495m**, or approximately
**S$2,525 psf**. That means about 9.96% annual appreciation over five years or
6.11% over eight years, before financing and holding costs. Urban Vista can
have good percentage upside from a disciplined low basis; huge absolute
quantum is mathematically unlikely without a very strong bull market.

## Scope and evidence rules

This is a property analysis, not a factor-audit report and not a unified
property ranking. Four evidence classes remain separate:

- **advertised asking inventory:** seller/agent marketing, not achieved price
  or confirmed same-day availability;
- **URA-derived Resale caveats:** achieved evidence, but voluntary and without
  full information on condition, incentives or legal configuration;
- **rental contracts:** anonymised area-band evidence, not the exact rent of a

- **scenario underwriting:** explicit assumptions, not forecasts.

## Project proposition

Urban Vista is a 99-year leasehold project whose lease commenced in 2012,
leaving approximately **84–85 years** at capture. It comprises **582
residential homes plus three commercial units** and completed around 2016.
Portal totals and labels should be checked for whether the commercial
component is included. The project's completion and developer reporting can be
cross-checked in this
[SGX announcement](https://links.sgx.com/FileOpen/AspialUV%20FaberNR%20-%20Final.ashx?AnncID=05FIIGF9GA0V2F1I&App=ArchiveAnnouncement&FileID=401256).

A representative straight-line OneMap coordinate measurement is approximately
**185 metres to Tanah Merah MRT**. This is not the same as the public walking
route, which portals commonly show around 380–390 metres depending on block
and station entrance. Tanah Merah serves the East-West Line and Changi Airport
branch, supporting a broad commuter and tenant catchment.

Urban Vista is close to MRT but not a fully integrated mixed-use development.
Its three commercial units are not equivalent to a destination mall or
Sceneca's direct station-linked proposition. The lease also began four years
before completion, which matters on longer exits even though there is no acute
five-to-eight-year lease cliff.

## Five- and eight-year capital-only test

The illustration uses a **S$930k purchase of a conventional 592 sqft unit** by
a Singapore citizen buying a first residential property.
It assumes dutiable value equals the entry price; actual BSD and ABSD apply to
the higher of purchase consideration or market value.

Assumptions:

- residential BSD under current marginal tiers: **S$22,500**;
- acquisition legal/due-diligence allowance: **S$5,000**;
- day-one capital basis: **S$957,500**;
- net exit proceeds equal **97.5% of exit price**, allowing 2.5% sale friction;
- no ABSD and disposal after SSD; and
- no mortgage interest, maintenance, property tax, renovation, rental income
  or opportunity cost.

Formula:

`capital profit = 0.975 × S$930k × (1 + annual growth)^years − S$957.5k`.

| Annual price growth | Five-year exit | Five-year capital profit/loss | Eight-year exit | Eight-year capital profit/loss |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0% | S$930k | -S$51k | S$930k | -S$51k |
| 2% | S$1.027m | +S$44k | S$1.090m | +S$105k |
| 3% | S$1.078m | +S$94k | S$1.178m | +S$191k |
| 4% | S$1.132m | +S$146k | S$1.273m | +S$283k |
| 5% | S$1.187m | +S$200k | S$1.374m | +S$382k |

| Profit target | Required exit | Approx. PSF on 592 sqft | Five-year annual growth | Eight-year annual growth |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Break-even | S$982k | S$1,659 | 1.10% | 0.68% |
| S$500k | S$1.495m | S$2,525 | 9.96% | 6.11% |
| S$1.0m | S$2.008m | S$3,391 | 16.64% | 10.10% |

For a Singapore citizen buying a **second** residential property, 20% ABSD
adds S$186,000. Simplified capital break-even becomes **S$1.173m**, or about
S$1,981 psf. It needs approximately 4.75% annual appreciation over five years
or 2.94% over eight years merely to recover entry and sale friction before
holding costs.

Current references:
[IRAS BSD](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-property/buying-or-acquiring-property/buyer%27s-stamp-duty-%28bsd%29),
[IRAS ABSD](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-property/buying-or-acquiring-property/additional-buyer%27s-stamp-duty-%28absd%29)
and
[IRAS SSD](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-property/selling-or-disposing-property/seller%27s-stamp-duty-%28ssd%29-for-residential-property).
For homes bought from 4 July 2025, current SSD generally applies at 16%, 12%,
8% and 4% in the first, second, third and fourth year. Exact dates and buyer
facts must be checked.

## Risks and limits to upside

- **Special-layout valuation traps:** loft, staircase, void, roof and balcony
  utility may not receive the same bank or resale value as conventional area.
- **Marketing bedroom inflation:** a platform or loft can be described as an
  extra room even when registered strata area and approved use differ.
- **Very wide achieved dispersion:** project or size-band averages can conceal
  severe unit-specific differences.
- **Lease began in 2012:** Urban Vista is older in tenure than its 2016
  completion date suggests.
- **Internal and external competition:** 582 homes and several immediate
  neighbouring projects provide close substitutes.
- **Future nearby supply:** the confirmed Bedok Rise parcel can create
  construction disruption and later competing stock.
- **Noise and privacy:** MRT/rail, New Upper Changi Road, station activity,
  depot and aircraft effects differ by stack.
- **MCST/ageing systems:** waterproofing, façades, lifts, pools, mechanical
  systems and sinking-fund adequacy require document review.
- **Low quantum is not free value:** compact homes can have a broad buyer pool
  but limited usable family space and many similar investor-owned exits.
- **ABSD/SSD:** policy friction can overwhelm expected capital gain.

## Due diligence before an option to purchase

1. Obtain exact unit, stack, floor, registered strata area, strata plan,
   approved floor plan and seller-authorised price.
2. Confirm same-day availability and deduplicate portal cards by agent
   authority, photographs, descriptions, tenancy and facing.
3. Use exact-format and same-floor caveats; do not mix single-level, loft,
   dual-key and penthouse products. Obtain a bank valuation.
4. For loft/penthouse stock, verify URA/BCA/MCST approvals, structural load,
   fire safety, measured useful area, staircase/void/roof allocation,
   waterproofing and bank treatment.
5. Inspect at several times for rail/road/aircraft noise, west sun, privacy,
   view obstruction and future GLS construction exposure.
6. Verify complete tenancy documents, stamp certificate, deposit, payment
   record, expiry/break clauses, utilities, inventory and repair duties.
7. Review MCST accounts, charges, AGM minutes, sinking fund, insurance,
   defects, special levies and major contracts.
8. Verify title, encumbrances, seller acquisition date/SSD and buyer-specific
   BSD, ABSD/remission, TDSR, financing, cash/CPF and rate stress.

## Final assessment

Urban Vista has **good percentage-value potential, healthy liquidity and useful
rental fallback**, particularly in conventional compact formats bought near
exact caveats. Its low entry ticket can make break-even and tenant coverage
more manageable than higher-priced neighbours.

It does **not** have evidence-backed huge absolute quantum potential at current
asks. A S$500,000 capital-only gain on a S$930k/592 sqft entry needs an exit
near S$2,525 psf, far beyond the captured conventional cohort. Even an
eight-year path requires sustained 6.11% annual growth before holding costs.

The opportunity is micro-selection: buy a verified conventional unit below or
near matching evidence, or pay a special-layout premium only after proving
approval, useful area, rental income and bankability. A low headline PSF on a
penthouse, unverified loft premium or 20% ABSD can turn apparent value into a
poor exit proposition.

This is a point-in-time research snapshot. Listings, transactions, rents,
project plans, tax rules and market conditions can change after capture and
must be refreshed immediately before an offer.
