## Decision

Limau Park has **moderate freehold own-stay potential but low-confidence huge
investment quantum**. Its public price discovery is exceptionally thin: the
committed latest-60-month dataset contains only three resale caveats, and the
current portal sweep appears to show only two unique 1,270 sqft seller leads
despite three raw cards.

The S$1.95m, 1,270 sqft advertisement at **S$1,535 psf** is the only current
lead close to achieved project evidence. A larger 1,518 sqft home achieved
S$2.30m, or S$1,515 psf, in May 2026. The current S$2.30m ask for only 1,270
sqft is S$1,811 psf and requires superior renovation, position and usable
layout; matching the latest total quantum while giving up about 248 sqft is
not an obvious bargain.

The evidence-led entry ladder for the advertised 1,270 sqft format is:

1. **At or below S$1.90m, or about S$1,496 psf:** potentially compelling if
   title, building condition, layout and future station route are clean.
2. **S$1.90m–S$1.98m:** defensible for ordinary liveable condition.
3. **S$1.98m–S$2.10m:** pay only for verified renovation, quiet aspect and
   strong common-property finances.
4. **Above S$2.10m:** thin investment case; S$2.30m needs exceptional
   attributes rather than freehold tenure alone.

At a representative S$1.95m purchase, BSD and a S$5,000 legal/technical
allowance produce about **S$2.0221m** of acquisition basis. A S$500,000
capital-only profit requires an exit near **S$2.587m** after 2.5% selling
friction—about 5.81% annual sale-price growth over five years or 3.60% over
eight years. Thin liquidity, old-building costs and an observed gross rent
around S$4,000–S$4,200 monthly make that upside possible but not
high-confidence.

Here, “huge quantum” means a credible large absolute-dollar profit after
purchase and sale friction, not a large floor area, freehold label or a portal
price projection.

## Scope and evidence rules

This report is a property analysis, not a factor-audit report, formal valuation
or unified property ranking. It keeps distinct:

- **raw asks**, which can be duplicated or stale;
- **achieved resale caveats**, which do not reveal condition or seller
  motivation;
- **rents by broad area band**, which are not net cash flow; and
- **capital scenarios**, which are assumptions rather than forecasts.

The official transaction reference is
[URA Property Market Information](https://eservice.ura.gov.sg/property-market-information/pmiResidentialTransactionSearch).
The public search covers the latest 60 months. Caveats are voluntarily lodged
with SLA and therefore not exhaustive.
[URA REALIS coverage and methodology](https://eservice.ura.gov.sg/reis/coverageandMethodology)
explains the wider data basis.

## Capital-only return test

The representative illustration assumes:

- purchase price: **S$1.950m**;
- **Singapore citizen (SC) buying a first residential property**, the SC
  first-property baseline with no ABSD;
- BSD: **S$67,100**, computed under
  [current IRAS residential BSD tiers](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-property/buying-or-acquiring-property/buyer%27s-stamp-duty-%28bsd%29);
- acquisition legal/technical allowance: **S$5,000**;
- acquisition basis: **S$2.0221m**;
- sale proceeds equal 97.5% of exit price after 2.5% selling friction; and
- no loan, maintenance, property tax, renovation, rent or vacancy in profit.

The scenario assumes dutiable value equals the S$1.950m entry price. Actual
BSD and ABSD are charged on the **higher of consideration or market value**,
so an apparently discounted purchase can attract duty on a higher value.

`capital-only profit = 0.975 × future sale price − S$2.0221m`

| Annual nominal sale-price change | Five-year exit / profit | Eight-year exit / profit |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1% | S$2.049m / **−S$24k** | S$2.112m / **S$37k** |
| 3% | S$2.261m / **S$182k** | S$2.470m / **S$386k** |
| 5% | S$2.489m / **S$404k** | S$2.881m / **S$787k** |

Friction-only break-even is approximately **S$2.074m**. A S$500,000
capital-only target requires about **S$2.587m**. The table excludes all holding
costs; actual net break-even is higher.

[IRAS's current ABSD table](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-property/buying-or-acquiring-property/additional-buyer%27s-stamp-duty-%28absd%29)
imposes 20% on a Singapore citizen buying a second residential property.
Adding S$390,000 raises break-even to approximately **S$2.474m** before
financing and holding costs, making an investment-only purchase materially
harder.

For homes bought on or after 4 July 2025,
[current IRAS SSD rules](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-property/selling-or-disposing-property/seller%27s-stamp-duty-%28ssd%29-for-residential-property)
apply 16%, 12%, 8% and 4% within the first four holding years. The five- and
eight-year cases assume sale after the exact fourth anniversary and no SSD
under today's rules.

## Due diligence before an offer

1. Confirm which raw cards are unique physical units; obtain current seller
   authority, exact block, floor, stack and dated viewing availability.
2. Reconcile 1,270 sqft and three-bedroom marketing against the subsidiary
   strata certificate, approved plan and option.
3. Separate enclosed usable area, terraces, voids and circulation; test the
   intended furniture plan.
4. Obtain AGM minutes, audited accounts, maintenance and sinking-fund balances,
   insurance, arrears, litigation and planned special levies.
5. Commission a building inspection focused on roof/wet-area waterproofing,
   pipes, electrical capacity, windows, pests, settlement and prior alterations.
6. Visit during rain, peak traffic, late evening and school periods to assess
   access, noise, privacy and drainage.
7. Walk the exact residential gate to Tanah Merah and future Bedok South
   station entrances; do not rely on straight-line distance.
8. Verify occupancy, tenancy, deposit transfer, rent, inventory and vacant
   possession in writing.
9. Obtain an independent bank valuation and loan indication before paying a
   non-refundable amount.
10. Recompute BSD, ABSD, SSD, CPF and loan treatment for the actual buyers and
    contract date; model renovation and all holding costs separately.

## Final assessment

Limau Park is interesting around **S$1.90m–S$1.98m for a verified 1,270 sqft
unit**, especially for a long-horizon own-stayer who values freehold, space and
low-rise form. The S$2.30m cards do not show huge potential merely because
their PSF is below some new launches. Thin caveats and weak rental support keep
the investment conclusion at moderate potential, not huge high-confidence
quantum.
