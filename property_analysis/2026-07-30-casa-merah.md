## Decision

Casa Merah is a space-led family proposition near Tanah Merah MRT. Its ordinary
three-bedroom homes are much larger than the compact formats in newer
neighbouring projects, achieved resale activity is healthy, and family-sized
PSF remains below younger Grandeur Park. The trade-off is higher total quantum,
an older 2006 lease and rising maintenance/exit risk as the project ages.

The strongest captured lead is the advertised **1,216 sqft three-bedroom at
S$1.833m**, or about S$1,507 psf. A February 2026 exact-size resale achieved
S$1.80m/S$1,480 psf, while the broader 1,200–1,399 sqft trailing cohort has a
S$1.93m/S$1,548 psf median. If the advertisement remains executable and the
unit has no material defect, poor facing or tenancy problem, it offers useful
entry discipline rather than a huge valuation error.

Evidence-backed buy boxes are:

1. **Best family-value cohort:** 1,216–1,259 sqft around
   **S$1.83m–S$1.98m**. Pay S$2.00m or above only for a demonstrably better
   floor, stack, renovation or tenancy.
2. **Larger 1,281–1,346 sqft three-bedroom:** approximately
   **S$1.93m–S$2.17m**. A captured S$2.35m/1,346 sqft ask is aggressive
   against a recent S$2.165m near-exact-size resale.
3. **Two-bedroom 947–990 sqft:** approximately **S$1.40m–S$1.55m**. The
   advertised S$1.60m/947 sqft is above both the cohort median and the latest
   same-size S$1.52m caveat.
4. **Four-bedroom 1,528–1,550 sqft:** exact-layout target around
   **S$2.35m–S$2.45m**. S$2.499m–S$2.53m asks carry a full premium.
5. **Penthouse/roof stock:** a 1,970 sqft S$2.55m ask has low-looking PSF but
   outdoor/void/staircase utility and waterproofing can differ from an
   ordinary apartment. It is not automatically the project's best value.

At a representative **S$1.92m purchase of 1,259 sqft**, a simplified
S$500,000 capital-only gain needs an exit near **S$2.554m**, or about
**S$2,029 psf**. This requires approximately 5.88% annual appreciation over
five years or 3.63% over eight years before financing and holding costs. The
eight-year target is plausible in a supportive market, but it is not
high-confidence “huge potential”: the exit occurs with a shorter lease, an
older building and a buyer quantum above S$2.5m.

This is a property analysis, not a factor-audit report and not a unified
property ranking. It distinguishes:

  or verified same-day availability;
- **URA-derived caveats filtered to Resale**, which are achieved evidence but

## Project proposition

Casa Merah is a 99-year leasehold condominium whose lease commenced in 2006,
leaving approximately **78–79 years** at capture. It has **556 residential
homes**, completed around 2009, and was developed by NTUC Choice Homes and Wing
Tai. Wing Tai includes Casa Merah in its
[residential portfolio](https://www.wingtaiasia.com/our-portfolio/residential/).

The project contains a relatively space-generous mix: public project records
show ordinary two-bedroom layouts mainly around 926–1,076 sqft,
three-bedroom layouts around 1,216–1,475 sqft and four-bedroom layouts from
about 1,528 sqft. This explains why Casa's total purchase price can exceed a
newer neighbour despite lower PSF.

A representative straight-line OneMap coordinate measurement is approximately
**188 metres to Tanah Merah MRT**. It is not a gate-to-platform route; public
portals commonly show about 450 metres walking depending on block and station
entrance. The East-West Line and Changi Airport branch are durable demand
anchors.

There is no immediate five-to-eight-year financing cliff at 78–79 years
remaining, but the lease is materially older than Urban Vista, The Glades,
Grandeur Park and Sceneca. A buyer should not price Casa as freehold or assume
that its current PSF discount is entirely recoverable.

## Five- and eight-year capital-only test

The illustration uses an **S$1.92m purchase of a conventional 1,259 sqft
three-bedroom** by a Singapore citizen buying a first residential property.
It assumes dutiable value equals the entry price; actual BSD and ABSD apply to
the higher of purchase consideration or market value.

Assumptions:

- residential BSD under current marginal tiers: **S$65,600**;
- acquisition legal/due-diligence allowance: **S$5,000**;
- day-one capital basis: **S$1,990,600**;
- net exit proceeds equal **97.5% of exit price**, allowing 2.5% sale friction;
- no ABSD and sale after SSD; and
- no financing, maintenance, property tax, renovation, rent or opportunity
  cost.

Formula:

`capital profit = 0.975 × S$1.92m × (1 + annual growth)^years − S$1.9906m`.

| Annual price growth | Five-year exit | Five-year capital profit/loss | Eight-year exit | Eight-year capital profit/loss |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0% | S$1.920m | -S$119k | S$1.920m | -S$119k |
| 2% | S$2.120m | +S$76k | S$2.249m | +S$203k |
| 3% | S$2.226m | +S$180k | S$2.433m | +S$381k |
| 4% | S$2.336m | +S$287k | S$2.626m | +S$571k |
| 5% | S$2.450m | +S$399k | S$2.836m | +S$775k |

| Profit target | Required exit | Approx. PSF on 1,259 sqft | Five-year annual growth | Eight-year annual growth |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Break-even | S$2.042m | S$1,622 | 1.24% | 0.77% |
| S$500k | S$2.554m | S$2,029 | 5.88% | 3.63% |
| S$1.0m | S$3.067m | S$2,436 | 9.82% | 6.03% |

For a Singapore citizen buying a **second** residential property, 20% ABSD
adds S$384,000. Simplified break-even becomes **S$2.435m**, about S$1,934 psf.
This needs approximately 4.87% annual appreciation over five years or 3.02%
over eight years merely to recover entry and sale friction before holding
costs.

Current references:
[IRAS BSD](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-property/buying-or-acquiring-property/buyer%27s-stamp-duty-%28bsd%29),
[IRAS ABSD](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-property/buying-or-acquiring-property/additional-buyer%27s-stamp-duty-%28absd%29)
and
[IRAS SSD](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-property/selling-or-disposing-property/seller%27s-stamp-duty-%28ssd%29-for-residential-property).
For acquisitions from 4 July 2025, current SSD generally applies at 16%, 12%,
8% and 4% within the first, second, third and fourth year. Exact dates, buyer
profile and current rules must be checked.

## Risks and limits to upside

- **Older leasehold basis:** a 2006 lease makes Casa materially older than the
  newest Tanah Merah alternatives.
- **Ageing-building costs:** lifts, façades, waterproofing, pools, pipes,
  mechanical systems and sinking-fund adequacy increasingly matter.
- **High total quantum:** generous space lowers PSF but narrows the buyer pool
  as ordinary family exits move above S$2.0m–S$2.5m.
- **Historical rerating is already realised:** median PSF has risen sharply
  since 2021.
- **Newer competition:** buyers may prefer smaller new units at similar total
  quantum, even when Casa offers more area.
- **Roof/penthouse denominator risk:** outdoor, staircase, void and roof area
  can make PSF look cheap while reducing usable interior value.
- **Station/road/aircraft environment:** noise, privacy, west sun and views
  vary by block and stack.
- **Future construction and supply:** the confirmed Bedok Rise parcel and
  other nearby developments can disrupt the environment before adding
  competing homes.
- **School-premium uncertainty:** 1 km eligibility is exact-address dependent
  and school policy can change.
- **ABSD/SSD and carrying cost:** policy friction can erase the investment case.

## Due diligence before an option to purchase

1. Obtain exact block/unit, stack, floor, registered area, floor plan and
   seller-authorised executable price.
2. Confirm same-day availability; deduplicate portal cards using agent
   authority, photographs, description, facing and tenancy.
3. Pull exact-size and same-floor-band caveats and obtain a bank valuation.
   Do not apply penthouse or smaller-unit PSF to unlike homes.
4. Inspect useful internal area, balcony/roof/void allocation, water ingress,
   ageing finishes, plumbing/electrical condition, west sun, privacy and noise.
5. Review MCST financial statements, sinking fund, maintenance charges, AGM
   minutes, insurance, defects, special levies and planned capital works.
6. Verify signed tenancy, stamp certificate, deposit, payment history, expiry,
   break/diplomatic clauses, inventory and reinstatement/repair obligations.
7. Verify exact-address school distance, title, encumbrances, seller
   acquisition date and seller SSD.
8. Recalculate buyer-specific BSD, ABSD/remission, financing, TDSR, CPF/cash,
   renovation budget, interest stress and planned holding period.

## Final assessment

Casa Merah has **good family-space value, healthy resale liquidity and moderate
long-horizon appreciation potential**. The clearest opportunity is a normal
1,216–1,259 sqft three-bedroom around S$1.83m–S$1.98m, after unit and MCST due
diligence.

The evidence does not support a blanket huge-potential claim. A five-year
S$500,000 capital-only gain from S$1.92m requires about S$2,029 psf and 5.88%
annual appreciation. Eight years reduces the hurdle to 3.63%, but the future
buyer faces an older project, shorter lease and exit above S$2.5m. Second-
property 20% ABSD raises break-even so sharply that the investment case becomes
weak before financing and holding costs.

Buy Casa for verified useful space at a disciplined basis, not because its PSF
looks low beside a compact new launch. At the lower captured family asks it can
be a sensible own-stay or defensive hold; at premium asks, unusual roof-area
denominators or with ABSD, expect ordinary or poor risk-adjusted returns rather
than huge quantum.

This report is a point-in-time snapshot. Advertisements, transactions, rents,
project plans, tax rules and market conditions can change after capture and
must be refreshed immediately before an offer.
