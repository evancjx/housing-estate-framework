# Grandeur Park Residences — quality-adjusted price index, ceiling test and revised buy box

Research captured: **2026-09-19 21:07:26 SGT (UTC+08:00)**  
Property: **Grandeur Park Residences, 1/3/5/7/9/11 Bedok South Avenue 3, Singapore**  
Analysis type: **property resale inventory, valuation and investment analysis**  
Status: **point-in-time market snapshot**  
Summary: **Updates the 2026-07-30 report. The project's own quality-adjusted price index is still rising (+4.9% in the year to 2026H1), but the OCR index turned negative in Q2 2026, and the exit price needed for a large capital gain now sits above every price ever achieved at Tanah Merah — including brand-new integrated stock.**

## Decision

This report **updates and partly corrects** the
[2026-07-30 Grandeur Park analysis](https://evancjx.github.io/housing-estate-framework/property-analysis-2026-07-30-grandeur-park-residences.html).
Two things must be said before anything else.

**First, the transaction evidence has not moved.** The committed URA extract
was pulled on 2026-07-25 and still ends with a **May 2026** Grandeur caveat.
The property portals that supplied the July advertised-inventory snapshot are
unreachable from this environment. So this is **not** a refreshed listings
report, and anyone treating the July asking prices as current is guessing.
What has changed is (a) the macro record for Q2 2026, which is now published,
and (b) how hard the same 168-caveat dataset has been worked.

**Second, my sharper modelling contradicts one thing the July report implied
and confirms another.**

- **Contradicted:** the July report treated 2026 as a near-stall ("partial 2026
  recorded S$2,018" against 2025's S$2,003, i.e. +0.7%). That comparison is
  mix-contaminated. A size- and floor-controlled hedonic index over all 168
  resale caveats (R² = 0.905) shows Grandeur at **124.7 in 2026H1 against
  118.9 in 2025H1 — up 4.9% year on year**, its strongest twelve-month reading
  since 2023H2. The project did not stall through May 2026.
- **Confirmed and hardened:** the July report's scepticism about "huge quantum
  upside" was right, and understated. The evidence for that has become
  arithmetically brutal — see the ceiling test below.

The revised read is therefore: **Grandeur Park is a good asset entering a bad
part of its own price cycle.** The quality-adjusted index compounding at
**5.03%/yr since 2021H2 but only 3.40%/yr over the last three years**, against
an OCR sub-index that fell **-0.1% q-o-q in Q2 2026** with OCR rents down
**-0.3%**, describes a project running ahead of its own market segment.

**Revised buy box**, from the hedonic model rather than cohort min/median/max,
for the conventional 883 sqft three-bedroom at September 2026:

| Floor band | Model fair value | Fair PSF |
|---|---:|---:|
| 01 to 05 | **S$1.750m** | S$1,982 |
| 06 to 10 | **S$1.791m** | S$2,029 |
| 11 to 15 | **S$1.821m** | S$2,063 |

That is a **better-specified** box than July's flat S$1.74m–S$1.80m "adjusted
for floor" — not a narrower one; it spans S$71k against July's S$60k, with a
higher ceiling. The gain is that the spread is now attributable to a measured
attribute rather than to cohort scatter. The floor adjustment is measured, not
hand-waved: **+2.3% for floors 6–10 and +4.1% for floors 11–15** over
floors 1–5, about S$70k top to bottom on an 883 sqft unit. Paying a
high-floor price for a low-floor unit is the single most common avoidable
error in this project, and it is worth roughly one year of price growth.

**The ceiling test is the reason to be cautious.** At a mid-floor S$1.791m
entry, a S$500,000 capital-only gain in five years requires an exit at
**S$2,736 psf**. For context, at this station:

- Grandeur's own trailing-12-month median resale PSF is **S$2,016**.
- The newest integrated stock at the same MRT, Sceneca Residence, last traded
  at **S$2,323 psf** (2026 sub-sale) and **S$2,341 psf** (2025 sub-sale).
- The Bedok Rise GLS site next to Tanah Merah MRT is underwritten at an
  illustrative **S$2,500 psf** in this repository's own
  [Bedok Rise analysis](https://evancjx.github.io/housing-estate-framework/property-analysis-2026-07-30-bedok-rise-gls-future-condominium.html),
  which treats S$2,400–S$2,550 as defensible and S$2,550–S$2,700 as requiring
  superior product evidence. External commentary after the land award spans
  roughly S$2,300–S$2,700. Nothing has been released by the developer.

So the S$500k five-year target asks a 2016-lease resale unit to exit **18%
above today's brand-new integrated stock at the same station**, and **9% above
the rate this repository's own Bedok Rise analysis uses for a condominium that
will not exist until 2029**. That is not a bull case. That is a category error.
Reject any pitch built on it.

An eight-year hold at **3.81%/yr** delivers the same S$500k and is defensible
— it is close to the project's realised 3.40%/yr three-year rate. **Eight years
is the honest holding period for this asset, not five.**

For a **second property**, 20% ABSD pushes simple break-even to **S$2,571 psf**
— i.e. a second-property buyer must resell in five years at roughly what a
brand-new 2029-completion condominium is expected to launch at today, merely
to recover acquisition and sale friction. **Do not buy this as a second
property for capital gain.**

## What changed since 2026-07-30

### The OCR turned negative — the July report did not mention it

URA's
[Q2 2026 real estate statistics](https://www.ura.gov.sg/news/media/pr26-57/)
show overall private residential prices **+0.5% q-o-q**, decelerating from
**+0.9%** in Q1. Underneath that, non-landed **Outside Central Region prices
fell 0.1%**, reversing a **+2.2%** Q1 gain. Non-landed rents rose 0.4% overall
but **OCR rents fell 0.3%**, and islandwide vacancy rose from **6.2% to 6.4%**
([ERA Q2 2026 review](https://www.era.com.sg/research-articles/2q-2026-ura-private-residential-report);
[CBRE commentary](https://www.cbre.com.sg/press-releases/commentary-on-the-flash-estimate-of-q2-2026-private-residential-property-price-index);
[URA Q2 flash estimate](https://www.ura.gov.sg/news/media/pr26-51/)).

Grandeur Park is OCR non-landed. **Both legs of its investment case — capital
value and rent — softened at the segment level in the quarter the local caveat
trail goes quiet.** The July report was written days after these figures became
available and did not reference them. That was a genuine gap, and it is the
most decision-relevant single fact added here.

### Competing new supply is larger than the July report described

The July report discussed Sceneca and the Bedok Rise parcel. It did not mention
that **District 16 absorbed 929 new-sale units in 2026 alone**, counted from
the raw URA extract `data/raw/ura/pmi_d16_2024-2026.csv`:

| Project | Street | 2026 new sales | 2026 median PSF | Months active |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| Vela Bay | Bayshore Walk | 376 | S$2,862 | 2026-04 to 2026-07 |
| Pinery Residences | Bedok Reservoir Road | 550 | S$2,548 | 2026-03 to 2026-06 |
| Bagnall Haus | Upper East Coast Road | 3 | S$2,540 | 2026-02 to 2026-04 |

**These three are not one precinct, and District 16 is a postal badge rather
than a market.** Only **Vela Bay** is in the Bayshore precinct. **Pinery
Residences sits in Tampines Planning Area** with a planned direct underground
link to Tampines West MRT on the Downtown Line — the repository's own
[Pinery analysis](https://evancjx.github.io/housing-estate-framework/property-analysis-2026-08-08-pinery-residences.html)
states this explicitly and warns against reading a portal's district badge as a
physical boundary. Bagnall Haus is an Upper East Coast infill.

None are Tanah Merah substitutes on walking distance. What they establish is
that D16-badged new-build demand is being absorbed **elsewhere in the east**, at
a **26%–42% PSF premium** to Grandeur's resale level, by buyers who are not
shopping at Tanah Merah. Read this as evidence about where the marginal new-build
buyer is going, not as direct competition for a Grandeur resale unit.

A counting note that matters for anyone reproducing these figures: the derived
`data/inputs/ura_private.csv` is built by concatenating the raw district extracts
and de-duplicating identical rows. For a new launch, many genuinely distinct units
sell at the same price, size, month and floor band, so de-duplication silently
collapses them — it returns 410 Pinery sales against 550 raw. Resale caveats
almost never collide this way, and Grandeur's 184 rows survive de-duplication
unchanged, so **every Grandeur figure in this report is unaffected**. New-launch
volumes here are therefore taken from the raw extract, and new-launch volumes
quoted from the derived file anywhere else should be treated as undercounts.

## Quality-adjusted price index — the correction to the July read

The July report compared annual median PSF, which mixes a 420 sqft studio and
a 1,744 sqft penthouse into one number and then reads the mix change as price
change. The size mix in 2026 was heavier — 2026 median *quantum* jumped to
S$1.540m from S$1.283m — which is why the July medians looked flat in PSF
while quantum looked explosive. Neither was a price signal.

Model: `log(PSF) ~ size fixed effects + floor-band fixed effects + half-year
fixed effects`, estimated by ordinary least squares on all **168 resale caveats
from 2021-07 to 2026-05**. **R² = 0.905.** Half-year dummies mean each period's
level is estimated free of size and floor mix.

| Half-year | Index (2021H2 = 100) | Caveats |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2021H2 | 100.0 | 10 |
| 2022H1 | 100.8 | 16 |
| 2022H2 | 107.8 | 17 |
| 2023H1 | 112.8 | 20 |
| 2023H2 | 113.3 | 16 |
| 2024H1 | 117.7 | 18 |
| 2024H2 | 118.1 | 19 |
| 2025H1 | 118.9 | 19 |
| 2025H2 | 122.1 | 17 |
| **2026H1** | **124.7** | **16** |

Compound rates:

| Window | Total | Annualised |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2021H2 → 2026H1 (4.5y) | +24.7% | **+5.03%** |
| 2023H1 → 2026H1 (3.0y) | +10.5% | **+3.40%** |
| 2023H2 → 2025H1 (1.5y) | +4.9% | +3.27% |
| 2025H1 → 2026H1 (1.0y) | **+4.9%** | +4.9% |

Read this honestly in both directions. The bear case that Grandeur has stalled
is **not supported** — the last twelve measured months were strong. The bull
case that 5%+ is the trend rate is **also not supported** — the headline
5.03%/yr is carried by 2022H2–2023H1, and the three-year rate is 3.40%.

**The right planning number for underwriting is 3.0%–3.5%/yr, not 5%.**

Their strata denominators include roof, void or airspace and the model has one
and two observations respectively. The July report's warning stands unchanged
and is the correct treatment.

## Revised capital-only test

Assumptions, stated: Singapore citizen, first residential property; dutiable
value equals entry price; current marginal BSD tiers; S$5,000 acquisition
legal and due-diligence allowance; net exit proceeds of **97.5%** of exit price
for agency, GST and legal friction; no ABSD; sale after the applicable SSD
period; and **no mortgage interest, maintenance, property tax, renovation,
rental income or opportunity cost**. Entry prices are the model fair values
above, not asking prices.

`capital profit = 0.975 × entry × (1 + g)^years − (entry + BSD + S$5,000)`

| Entry unit | Entry | BSD | Break-even exit | Break-even PSF |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 883 sqft, floor 01–05 | S$1.750m | S$57,100 | S$1.859m | S$2,105 |
| 883 sqft, floor 06–10 | S$1.791m | S$59,150 | S$1.903m | S$2,155 |
| 883 sqft, floor 11–15 | S$1.821m | S$60,650 | S$1.935m | S$2,191 |

**This table is the report.** Read the PSF columns against the ceiling section:
S$2,736 psf is 18% above the newest integrated stock's last sub-sale; S$3,025

*dollar* gain without a fantastical PSF, because the dollar target is fixed and
the area is small. **If the objective is a S$300k–S$500k dollar gain, the
883–893 sqft cohort is the only rational vehicle in this project**, and the
holding period is eight years.

| **3.4%** (realised 3y rate) | S$2.117m (2,397 psf) | **+S$209k** | S$2.340m (2,650 psf) | **+S$427k** |
| 5.0% | S$2.286m (2,589 psf) | +S$374k | S$2.646m (2,997 psf) | +S$725k |

The central case — the project's own realised three-year rate of 3.40% — gives
**+S$209k over five years and +S$427k over eight**, before financing and
holding costs. That is a sound, unspectacular outcome. It is also the honest
one.

**Second property, 20% ABSD:** adds S$358,200 at a S$1.791m entry. Basis
becomes S$2,213,350 and break-even becomes **S$2.270m, or S$2,571 psf** —
requiring 4.86%/yr over five years or 3.01%/yr over eight *merely to recover
friction*, before a single dollar of profit and before holding costs. The 8-year
figure is achievable at the realised rate; the 5-year one is not, on this
evidence.

## Estate framework context

The repository's own Provision model scores **BEDOK at 4.18, band B+,
archetype B with high confidence** (`data/outputs/master_output.csv`,
model 2.1.0). Component detail is strong on the things that make a family home
work — amenities 5.0, health 5.0, infrastructure 5.0, childcare 5.0, community
5.0, sport 5.0, hawker 4.8, connectivity 4.45, schools 4.4 — and weak on two:
**density 1.0 and flood proximity 1.0**, with noise at 3.0.

The flood component is an inverse distance-to-nearest-PUB-flood-prone-location
score at **estate** resolution. A 1.0 means Bedok sits close to a listed
location; it says nothing about Bedok South Avenue 3 specifically. Treat it as
a prompt to check drainage and historical flash-flood reports on the actual
approach roads, not as a property-level finding.

The Liveability matrix puts BEDOK at **D_T0 = 0.832, rising to 1.00 at T5 and
T15**, with every persona gap labelled `over_equipped` at T0 and `matched` at
T5. In the framework's own terms: Bedok's provision today exceeds what its
current demand profile requires, and the T5 additions close the gap rather than
extending the lead. Lease band is **C** (2.0), reflecting the estate's HDB lease
profile, not Grandeur's own 88-year balance.

Provision is supply-side and comparable; Liveability is demand-side and
person-relative. Neither produces a unified ranking, and neither should be read
as a price forecast.

## Risks and limits to upside

- **The segment turned before the project did.** OCR non-landed prices and
  rents both fell in Q2 2026. Grandeur's last positive index point is built on
  January–April caveats.
- **The convergence trade is mostly spent.** At 87% of the newest integrated
  stock's sub-sale PSF, the remaining room to the observable ceiling is ~15%.
- **The nearest high-volume comparable is down.** The Glades, 44 caveats and
  beside the same station, fell 1.5% over the last twelve months — on a 2013
  lease against Grandeur's 2016, so three years further along.
- **Dollar-gain targets require impossible PSF.** S$500k in five years needs
  S$2,736 psf on an 883 sqft unit — above anything ever achieved at this
  station, new or old.
- **New supply is both benchmark and substitute.** Bedok Rise (~380 units, next
  to Tanah Merah) is the direct one. The 929 D16-badged new-sale units absorbed
  in 2026 are mostly *not* — 550 are Pinery in Tampines Planning Area and 376
  are Vela Bay at Bayshore — but they show where the marginal new-build buyer in
  the east is going, at a 26%–42% PSF premium.
- **Thin cohorts.** The 1,238 sqft (4 caveats) and 1,453 sqft (11) grids carry
  wide error; 904 sqft and 1,744 sqft are not modellable at all.
- **Internal competition.** 720 homes with heavily repeated layouts — 27 caveats
  at 667 sqft, 20 at 883, 17 at 420 — means several near-identical sellers can
  compete at your exit.
- **Leasehold.** About 88 years remaining is ample for this hold, but the estate
  lease band is C and the asset cannot hold a freehold-scarcity narrative.
- **Micro-location.** Station, rail, road and aircraft noise, west sun, privacy
  and blocked views vary by stack; estate-level noise scores 3.0 and flood
  proximity 1.0.
- **MCST and defects.** Façade, waterproofing, pool, lift, air-conditioning and
  sinking-fund liabilities can offset the appeal of a young project.
- **Policy cost.** 20% ABSD makes the second-property case fail on a five-year
  view; four-year SSD removes short-term flexibility entirely.
- **Model limits.** The hedonic cannot see condition, renovation, facing,
  tenancy, stack or view. R² of 0.873 on the fair-value grid means roughly 13%
  of PSF variation is exactly the stuff you inspect for. The grid also assumes
  the 2021–2026 trend continues linearly to September 2026, which is precisely
  what the Q2 2026 OCR reading puts in doubt.

## Due diligence before an option to purchase

1. **Re-pull the caveats first.** This report's transaction evidence ends in
   May 2026. Obtain June–September 2026 caveats for Grandeur and The Glades
   before making any offer; the June–July gap must be explained before it is
   interpreted.
2. Confirm exact block, unit, floor, stack, strata area, floor plan and
   seller-authorised executable price, then locate the unit in the fair-value
   grid by **exact size and floor band**.
3. Reconcile duplicate advertisements by photographs, tenancy, facing and agent
   authority; obtain same-day proof of availability.
4. Pull exact-size, same-floor-band and preferably same-stack caveats, then
   obtain a bank valuation. Where the model and the bank disagree, the bank
   decides your financing.
5. Inspect useful internal area, balcony/patio/void allocation, west sun,
   privacy, station/rail/road/aircraft noise and construction exposure at
   several times of day. Check drainage and flash-flood history on the approach
   roads.
6. For a tenanted purchase, inspect the complete signed tenancy, stamp
   certificate, deposit, rent history, expiry, diplomatic and break clauses,
   inventory and repair obligations — and re-price the yield against Q3 2026
   rents, not June 2026 rents.
7. Review MCST accounts, AGM minutes, maintenance and sinking-fund amounts,
   insurance, defects, special levies, major contracts and renovation records.
8. Verify title, caveats, encumbrances, seller acquisition date and seller SSD.
9. Recalculate buyer-specific BSD, ABSD and remission, financing, TDSR, CPF and
   cash needs, and interest-rate stress immediately before offer.
10. Track the Bedok Rise launch. Its actual released pricing — not the
    S$2,300–S$2,700 estimate — will reset every resale comparable at this
    station, upward or downward.

## Final assessment

Grandeur Park Residences remains the **price leader and the best defensive
asset** in the Tanah Merah resale cluster, and its quality-adjusted price index
was still rising at **+4.9% year on year through 2026H1**. The July report's
implied stall was a mix artefact and is corrected here.

But the July report's central judgement — that there is no evidence-backed
"huge quantum" upside — survives this update **and gets stronger**. The exit
PSF required for a large five-year dollar gain now sits above every price ever
achieved at this station, including brand-new integrated stock and the
consensus launch estimate for a condominium that will not complete until 2029.

What to do with that:

- **Buy at or below the model fair value for the exact size and floor band**,
  not at a cohort median. The floor adjustment is +2.3% and +4.1%, and it is
  worth about S$70k on an 883 sqft unit.
- **Prefer 883–893 sqft** if the objective is a dollar gain. Prefer 420–474 sqft
  if the objective is yield. The 560–667 sqft middle does neither especially
  well.
- **Underwrite at 3.0%–3.5%/yr, not 5%.** That is the project's realised
  three-year rate and it produces roughly **+S$209k over five years and +S$427k
  over eight** on a mid-floor 883 sqft entry, before financing and holding
  costs.
- **Plan for eight years, not five.** SSD runs four years, the segment has just
  turned, and the eight-year arithmetic is the only version of the S$500k target
  that is not a fantasy.
- **Do not buy this as a second property for capital gain.** ABSD break-even
  alone requires 4.86%/yr over five years.
- **Refresh the caveats before offering.** The single most important number in
  this report — whether the June–July 2026 caveat gap was a lull or a turn — is
  one that this environment could not reach.
