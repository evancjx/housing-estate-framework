# The Tanamera — resale inventory and quantum analysis

Research captured: **2026-07-30 22:48:38 SGT (UTC+08:00)**  
Property: **The Tanamera, Tanah Merah Kechil Road, Singapore**  
Analysis type: **property resale inventory, valuation and investment analysis**  
Status: **point-in-time market snapshot**

## Decision

The Tanamera is an appealing **large-format, near-MRT own-stay property**, but
it is not a high-confidence huge-quantum investment at ordinary current asks.
Its 288 homes, basement parking and low-density family layouts are difficult
to replace at the same total price. Against that, the lease began in 1990,
approximately 62–63 years remain, and three attempted collective-sale
initiatives have not produced a sale.

For a normal approximately 1,324 sqft three-bedroom, achieved prices since July
2025 range from S$1.529m to S$1.681m. Current public asks around S$1.65m,
S$1.70m and S$1.75m therefore span fair to premium territory. A disciplined
buyer should centre negotiations around **S$1.57m–S$1.63m**, not capitalise
en-bloc speculation.

The conditional entry ladder is:

1. **At or below S$1.56m:** compelling for a standard 1,324 sqft unit, subject
   to condition, floor, facing and MCST checks.
2. **S$1.57m–S$1.65m:** broadly fair and evidence-backed.
3. **S$1.66m–S$1.70m:** requires a superior floor, renovation, view or
   vacant-possession advantage.
4. **Above S$1.70m:** premium pricing against the latest matching caveats.

The 947–958 sqft two-bedroom segment must be underwritten separately. Its
latest achieved evidence is approximately S$1.10m–S$1.18m, making a current
S$1.29m ask ambitious without exceptional unit attributes.

At a S$1.60m representative entry, eight-year 3% annual price growth gives
about **S$322k capital-only profit** before financing and holding costs. A S$1m
capital-only profit requires an exit near **S$2.723m**. Remaining lease,
buyer-pool narrowing and the failed collective-sale history make that a bull
case rather than a base case. Potential quantum is **moderate, not huge**.

## Scope and evidence rules

This is a property analysis, not a factor-audit report or unified ranking.
Advertised asks, achieved caveats and analytical scenarios are treated as
different evidence:

- A portal card proves marketing, not continued availability or seller
  acceptance.
- A caveat proves an achieved transaction but not identical renovation,
  facing, defects or incentives.
- A scenario tests mathematics and is not a forecast.

The primary achieved-price reference is
[URA Property Market Information](https://eservice.ura.gov.sg/property-market-information/pmiResidentialTransactionSearch).
Caveat lodging is voluntary, so the public record is not exhaustive. Exact
strata area and floor plan outrank portal bedroom tags.

## Project proposition

[GuocoLand's official project page](https://www.guocoland.com.sg/SG/The-Tanamera.asp)
states 288 homes, unit sizes from 900 to 2,000 sqft, completion in 1994, and
facilities including a putting green, pool, jogging circuit, tennis, squash,
playground and basement parking. It calls the development three blocks, while
current property databases display several postal-address blocks. Buyers
should settle the legal block and strata identity from the title rather than
portal labels.

The captured URA records state a 99-year lease commencing in 1990. Roughly
**62–63 years** remain at the research date and only about 54–55 would remain
after an eight-year hold. Financing and CPF use depend on buyer age, lease and
then-current rules; this is not automatically “unfinanceable,” but the future
buyer pool deserves an explicit discount.

The value proposition is usable internal area and near-station convenience,
not modern facilities or lease freshness. Legacy floor plates can have
generous rooms but also less efficient circulation, large balconies or
maintenance-heavy elements. Inspect the exact plan.

## Rental fallback

The
[99.co project rental ledger](https://www.99.co/singapore/condos-apartments/the-tanamera)
shows recent 1,300–1,400 sqft three-bedroom rents largely around
**S$3,900–S$4,700 per month**, with unit-level dispersion. The latest
1,500–1,600 sqft evidence is around S$4,400, while 900–1,000 sqft two-bedrooms
have achieved roughly S$3,300–S$3,700.

A S$4,200 monthly proxy against a S$1.60m purchase is only **3.15% gross**
before vacancy, maintenance, agent fees, property tax, repairs, insurance,
furnishing and finance. Rental demand helps holding power but does not justify
paying an en-bloc or renovation premium without evidence.

## Collective-sale evidence

[CNA's review of repeated attempts](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/today/ground-up/years-after-en-bloc-boom-tensions-neighbours-failed-sale-bids-4647306)
reports that The Tanamera did not launch a collective sale after attempts in
2017, 2019 and 2023; the latest effort failed to secure the required 80%
consent. Earlier
[Business Times reporting](https://www.businesstimes.com.sg/property/residential/tanamera-joins-en-bloc-hopefuls)
described a roughly 236,778 sqft site and possible reserve price above S$500m.

That history confirms redevelopment interest but, more importantly, a repeated
owner-consensus hurdle. Under
[Strata Titles Boards guidance](https://www.stratatb.gov.sg/how-do-i-apply/matters-under-ltsa/),
developments at least ten years old generally require 80% by share value and
strata area before the statutory sale process. No current tender was verified.
The base case assigns **zero en-bloc value**.

## Underwriting and future quantum

The representative case is a standard approximately 1,324 sqft three-bedroom
at **S$1.60m**. BSD is **S$49,600** under the current
[IRAS residential BSD tiers](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-property/buying-or-acquiring-property/buyer%27s-stamp-duty-%28bsd%29).
The illustration assumes dutiable value equals entry price; actual BSD and
ABSD apply to the higher of purchase consideration or market value.
The no-ABSD case assumes a Singapore citizen buying a first residential
property.

`capital-only profit = exit price × 0.975 − entry price − BSD − S$5,000 acquisition legal/technical allowance`

The assumed 2.5% selling friction and fixed S$5,000 acquisition allowance are
included. The scenarios exclude any other legal fees, financing, property tax,
maintenance, renovation, rent, vacancy and opportunity cost. The
second-property column also deducts **20% of entry**, the current
[SC second-property ABSD rate](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-property/buying-or-acquiring-property/additional-buyer%27s-stamp-duty-%28absd%29).

| Hold / annual price growth | Exit quantum | SC first-property capital-only profit | SC second-property after 20% ABSD |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 years / 0% | S$1.600m | -S$95k | -S$415k |
| 5 years / 2% | S$1.767m | S$68k | -S$252k |
| 5 years / 3% | S$1.855m | S$154k | -S$166k |
| 5 years / 4% | S$1.947m | S$243k | -S$77k |
| 8 years / 0% | S$1.600m | -S$95k | -S$415k |
| 8 years / 2% | S$1.875m | S$173k | -S$147k |
| 8 years / 3% | S$2.027m | S$322k | S$2k |
| 8 years / 4% | S$2.190m | S$480k | S$160k |

The SC first-property capital-only break-even is about 1.2% annual growth over
five years or 0.7% over eight, before holding costs. Current
[SSD rules](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-property/selling-or-disposing-property/seller%27s-stamp-duty-%28ssd%29-for-residential-property)
apply for four years to purchases from 4 July 2025; the modelled exits avoid
SSD only if holding dates and rules remain as assumed.

## Catalysts

- Near-MRT family floor plates that are expensive to reproduce in newer
  projects.
- Immediate station-area retail and public-realm improvement.
- Low project density and established mature-estate services.
- Entry materially below S$1.60m could create useful downside protection.

## Risks

- Only approximately 62–63 lease years remain, narrowing to around 54–55 after
  eight years.
- Repeated failed collective-sale initiatives demonstrate owner-consensus
  risk; never pay an en-bloc premium.
- Older lifts, waterproofing, façades, roofs, pipes, basement carpark and
  recreational facilities may need major capital works.
- Nine annual resales are adequate but not deep liquidity, especially for
  1,500 sqft-plus units.
- Newer station-area stock can cap PSF rerating and reset buyer expectations.
- Portal block descriptions and listing duplication require title-level
  verification.

## Due diligence before an offer

1. Review title, lease dates, strata area, floor plan and share value.
2. Read MCST accounts, AGM and collective-sale minutes; test sinking-fund
   adequacy, major works, insurance claims and special levies.
3. Inspect roofs, basement, pool, water ingress, plumbing, façade, lifts and
   windows.
4. Verify every renovation approval, tenancy term and vacant-possession
   obligation.
5. Obtain written bank valuation and exact-size URA caveats. Test future
   financing and CPF implications for plausible buyer ages rather than relying
   on a generic lease rule.

## Final assessment

The Tanamera can be a rational **large-format own-stay or patient rental
purchase around S$1.57m–S$1.63m for 1,324 sqft**, especially if condition is
good and the MCST is well funded. It is not a redevelopment arbitrage. Entry
above S$1.70m for an ordinary unit requires too much future appreciation from
an ageing lease and a historically difficult collective-sale process.
