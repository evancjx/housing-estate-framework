## Decision

The Poiz Residences is a strong defensive city-fringe property with direct MRT,
retail and rental advantages, but the evidence does not support
**high-confidence huge capital appreciation** from ordinary current asking
prices. Its integration premium is established, most of the Bidadari uplift has
already been realised, and the 99-year lease began in 2014. The opportunity is
to buy a specific layout below matching caveats, not to assume that every Poiz
unit is undervalued.

The strongest conventional investment lead in the captured inventory is the
advertised **581 sqft two-bedroom/one-bathroom unit at S$1.20m**, or
approximately S$2,065 psf. It is about 4% below the latest two exact-size
caveats at S$1.25m and discloses an existing S$3,600 monthly tenancy to November
2026. If the unit, tenancy and seller price are genuine, this is useful entry
headroom rather than a huge valuation error.

Evidence-backed buy boxes are:

1. **Best balanced investment lead:** 581 sqft 2BR/1BA at no more than
   approximately S$1.20m.
2. **Best compact family/own-stay lead:** 840 sqft 3BR around
   S$1.90m–S$1.93m.
3. **Best larger family value:** 1,184 sqft 3BR around S$2.60m–S$2.65m.
4. **Best very-low-quantum lead:** 441 sqft at S$860k–S$880k, subject to
   confirmation that the lower advertisement remains live.
5. **Best income-oriented ticket:** conventional 420 sqft stock around
   S$820k–S$835k; the advertised S$810k is old and merely matches a recent
   caveat, so it is not automatically a bargain.
6. **Special-layout lead only:** 592 sqft roof/loft stock around S$968k. Its
   low PSF reflects outdoor or void area and cannot be compared naively with a
   normal 592 sqft interior.

Clear poor-value entries include 441 sqft near S$990k–S$1.00m, 581 sqft near
S$1.28m–S$1.35m, 936 sqft at approximately S$2.459m, 1,109 sqft at S$2.79m and
1,184 sqft at S$2.78m unless an exceptional floor, view, condition or tenancy
justifies the premium.

Here, "quantum potential" means whether today's total purchase price leaves
credible room for future resale and absolute dollar appreciation. The
S$810k–S$1.20m compact units have a broad potential buyer and tenant pool, but
low absolute quantum is not the same as undervaluation. A low ticket also makes
a very large absolute profit mathematically harder.

## Scope and evidence rules

This is a property analysis, not a factor-audit report and not a unified
property ranking. It separates three evidence classes:

- **Advertised asking inventory:** evidence that an agent is marketing a unit,
  not proof that it remains available or that the seller will accept the
  headline price.
- **Achieved transactions and rents:** stronger market evidence from lodged
  caveats and rental contracts, but without complete information on condition,
  renovation, facing, incentives or exact tenancy terms.
- **Scenario underwriting:** explicit analytical assumptions, not a forecast
  or guaranteed return.

The primary achieved-price source is
[URA Property Market Information](https://eservice.ura.gov.sg/property-market-information/pmiResidentialTransactionSearch).
URA's public search was last updated on 24 July 2026 and covers the latest 60
months. Resale and subsale observations are based on caveats lodged with SLA;
caveats are voluntary and therefore not exhaustive. URA describes the broader
transaction methodology in
[REALIS coverage and methodology](https://eservice.ura.gov.sg/reis/coverageandMethodology).

Exact-size calculations in this report use the captured public
EdgeProp/URA-derived transaction export, reconciled against the
[99.co Poiz project record](https://www.99.co/singapore/condos-apartments/the-poiz-residences),
[EdgeProp Poiz project record](https://www.edgeprop.sg/condo-apartment/the-poiz-residences)
and

## Project proposition

[MCC Land's official project page](https://www.mcc.sg/project-detail?id=Poiz)
describes a mixed-use development completed in 2018 with 731 homes, eight
18-storey residential flats and a 99-year tenure. The residential addresses
sit above The Poiz Centre and connect directly into the Potong Pasir MRT
environment on the North East Line.

The lease commenced on 17 November 2014, leaving approximately **87.3 years**
at the research date. There is no acute five-to-ten-year lease problem, but a
future buyer should not value the property as freehold:
[EdgeProp project details](https://www.edgeprop.sg/condo-apartment/the-poiz-residences).

The site economics are historically favourable rather than a current
replacement-cost guarantee. URA awarded the 16,149.4 sqm mixed-use site to MCC
Land for S$471.618m in August 2014, equivalent to roughly S$775 psf per plot
ratio by calculation:
[URA tender award](https://www.ura.gov.sg/Corporate/Media-Room/Media-Releases/pr14-52).
That low historical land basis helped original purchasers; it does not create a
new discount for a resale buyer entering around S$2,000–S$2,300 psf.

[The Poiz Centre's current directory](https://thepoizcentre.com.sg/shops.html)
shows a practical daily-retail mix including FairPrice, pharmacy and personal
care, medical/dental, fitness, enrichment, food and services. It is useful
residential infrastructure, but it is a comparatively small strata-retail
podium rather than an institutional destination mall such as Woodleigh Mall or
NEX. Secondary 2019 reporting described 84 strata shops and weaker traffic in
some internal alleys:
[EdgeProp Potong Pasir retail review](https://www.edgeprop.sg/property-news/revitalising-potong-pasir%E2%80%99s-private-property-market).
Fragmented retail ownership and tenant mix are therefore risks to inspect, not
reasons to capitalise the podium like a major mall.

## Five-year capital-return test

The following illustration tests the strongest conventional candidate: a
S$1.20m purchase of 581 sqft by a Singapore citizen buying a first residential
property.

Assumptions:

- buyer's stamp duty: S$32,600;
- purchase legal cost: S$3,000;
- sale legal cost: S$3,000;
- selling commission: 2% plus 9% GST, or 2.18% of exit price;
- no ABSD;
- no mortgage interest, maintenance, property tax, vacancy or rental income;
  and
- sale after the SSD holding period.

Current duty references:
[IRAS buyer's stamp duty](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-property/buying-or-acquiring-property/buyer%27s-stamp-duty-%28bsd%29),
[IRAS ABSD](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-property/buying-or-acquiring-property/additional-buyer%27s-stamp-duty-%28absd%29)
and
[IRAS SSD](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-property/selling-or-disposing-property/seller%27s-stamp-duty-%28ssd%29-for-residential-property).

| Five-year price growth | Exit price | Approx. capital-only profit/loss |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0% p.a. | S$1.200m | -S$65k |
| 2% p.a. | S$1.325m | +S$57k |
| 3% p.a. | S$1.391m | +S$122k |
| 4% p.a. | S$1.460m | +S$190k |
| 5% p.a. | S$1.532m | +S$260k |

The approximate capital-only break-even exit is **S$1.266m**, 5.5% above
purchase price. Producing S$300,000 of capital-only profit requires an exit
near **S$1.573m**, or approximately **S$2,707 psf**. That requires about 5.56%
annual price growth for five years, is 26% above the latest exact-size
S$2,151 psf caveat and is 13% above the existing project PSF record. This is a
bull case, not a reasonable base-case promise.

The low-ticket 420 sqft segment is even less likely to generate a huge absolute
profit. A S$820k purchase needs an exit near S$1.171m, or approximately
S$2,787 psf, to produce S$300,000 under the same simplified cost assumptions.
That requires about 7.4% annual price growth for five years.

For a Singapore citizen buying a second residential property, 20% ABSD adds
S$240,000 to the S$1.20m entry. Capital break-even rises to approximately
**S$1.512m**, or 26.0% above purchase price. This requires about 4.72% annual
appreciation for five years merely to recover transaction costs before
financing and holding costs.

Purchases made on or after 4 July 2025 generally must be held for more than four
years to avoid SSD. Under current rates, disposal within the first, second,
third or fourth year attracts 16%, 12%, 8% or 4% SSD respectively. Buyer
nationality, residency, existing property count, ownership structure and any
remission eligibility must be checked against current IRAS rules.

## Underwriting range

This is an analytical range, not a forecast:

| Scenario | Annual appreciation | Approx. five-year price change | Conditions |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Bear | -2% to 0% | -10% to 0% | RCR weakness, financing or employment shock, abundant resale/new supply, compact-unit competition |
| Base | 2% to 3% | +10% to +16% | Stable rents and employment, continued MRT/integration demand, market-like compounding |
| Bull | 4% to 5% | +22% to +28% | Strong market-wide growth, scarce matching-layout resale supply and sustained newer-project price umbrella |

The base case is below the rough 3.6% launch-to-current gross CAGR because a
current buyer enters after most of the integration and Bidadari rerating. It is
also consistent with a mature project whose compact segment has recently
plateaued.

URA reported overall private residential prices up 0.5% in Q2 2026, but RCR
non-landed prices down 1.2% quarter on quarter. It also expects about 60,600
private homes including ECs to complete over the next few years:
[URA Q2 2026 statistics](https://www.ura.gov.sg/news/media/pr26-57/).
This does not forecast a Poiz decline, but it argues against assuming effortless
high appreciation.

## Catalysts and optionality

- **Bidadari healthcare completion:** MOH says Bidadari Polyclinic and its
  co-located nursing home are scheduled to complete by 2027. This improves the
  wider area's services but is nearer Woodleigh and is a modest amenity
  catalyst, not a reason to pay a large Poiz premium.
  [MOH 2026 capacity update](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/enhancing-quality-and-coordination-of-care/).
- **Kallang River connectivity:** URA says the next Bishan-to-City phase,
  including the PIE cycling bridge, is targeted for completion by 2027. Better
  walking and cycling continuity is positive for Potong Pasir.
  [URA Kallang River plans](https://www.ura.gov.sg/land-planning/shaping-our-city/identity-corridors/kallang-river/).
- **Potong Pasir waterfront proposals:** Master Plan 2025 includes concepts for
  enhanced landscaping, seating, community spaces and an outdoor-learning
  environment at the Potong Pasir–St Andrew's river stretch. These are planning
  proposals with uncertain unit-level value, not bankable cash flows.
- **Bidadari build-out is delivered:** HDB announced on 7 January 2026 that
  construction of the final four Bidadari BTO projects was complete. The
  resulting population and amenity catchment supports Poiz retail and rental
  demand, but the major development story has crystallised rather than
  remaining an unpriced future catalyst.
  [HDB completion announcement](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/hdb-pulse/news/2026/final-four-projects-in-bidadari-completed).
- **School access:** public project records place St Andrew's School (Junior)
  and Cedar Primary within 1 km of some Poiz addresses. St Andrew's Junior is a
  boys' school, and distance eligibility is address-specific. Verify the exact
  block in
  [MOE SchoolFinder](https://www.moe.gov.sg/schoolfinder) before attributing a
  school premium.

The largest old catalysts—Poiz Centre, Potong Pasir MRT integration, Bidadari
housing, Woodleigh Mall and the new district population—are substantially
delivered. Remaining improvements support liveability and rental demand but
should not be modelled as a second greenfield rerating.

## Risks and limits to upside

- **Integration premium is already visible.** Poiz trades above older
  Sennett Residence and close to newer The Tre Ver despite its compact unit
  mix.
- **Many close substitutes:** OpenAgent identifies 324 one-bedroom homes,
  approximately 44.3% of the project. A compact seller can face several
  near-identical listings at exit.
- **Future first-owner supply:** approximately 67% of units remain with first
  owners in the OpenAgent snapshot. Their lower historical cost base can allow
  them to accept prices that still produce a gain.
- **Finite tenure:** approximately 87.3 years remain. This is ample for a
  normal hold but provides no freehold scarcity premium and will matter more on
  distant exits.
- **Newer integrated competition:** The Woodleigh Residences offers a newer
  NEL-integrated alternative in Bidadari. Park Colonial and other near-MRT stock
  also create an upper price comparison rather than a guaranteed convergence
  target.
- **Special-layout PSF traps:** 592 sqft and roof/penthouse strata areas can
  include outdoor or void space. Low PSF may simply reflect less useful
  interior area, higher upkeep and a narrower financing or buyer audience.
- **Current higher-PSF caveats are thin:** the S$2,390 record and recent larger
  layout gains are individual transactions, not deep evidence at every floor
  and facing.
- **Nearby future launch competition:** URA awarded the Kallang Close
  residential site in April 2026; tender material estimates approximately 470
  future homes. It is not immediate Potong Pasir stock, but it will compete for
  part of the wider RCR buyer and tenant pool.
  [URA Kallang Close award](https://www.ura.gov.sg/news/media/pr26-29/).
- **Mixed-use externalities:** MRT, road, retail and service/loading activity
  improve convenience but can create noise, odour, traffic, privacy and
  security trade-offs.
- **Unit-specific losses dominate project quality:** west sun, blocked view,
  low-floor privacy, water ingress, tenancy terms, renovation defects, MCST
  liabilities and bank valuation are not captured by project averages.
- **Policy and carrying costs:** ABSD can destroy the investment case for a
  second-property buyer, while SSD makes a short resale strategy expensive.

## Due diligence before an option to purchase

For the S$1.20m 581 sqft candidate, obtain:

1. Exact block, unit number, floor, stack and a written seller-authorised price.
   Confirm whether S$1.20m is executable or only a marketing lead.
2. Same-day proof that the unit remains available. Match duplicated portal
   pages to one physical property before counting competing supply.
3. A bank valuation and caveats for the same stack and floor band, not only
   project-wide or bedroom averages.
4. The complete signed tenancy agreement, deposit transfer, S$3,600 payment
   history, November 2026 expiry, break/diplomatic clauses, inventory and
   repair obligations.
5. Daytime, peak-hour and evening inspections for MRT/road noise, mall loading,
   food odour, west sun, privacy, lift traffic and view obstruction.
6. MCST maintenance and sinking-fund amounts, AGM minutes, insurance, defects,
   water ingress, special levies and renovation approvals.
7. Title/caveat checks, seller acquisition date, encumbrances and any seller
   SSD constraint.
8. Buyer-specific ABSD, financing, TDSR, cash/CPF needs, interest-rate stress,
   cash-flow buffer and a hold longer than four years.

For a roof/loft or penthouse layout, also inspect the strata plan, measured
internal versus outdoor area, waterproofing history, roof responsibility,
valuation comparables and whether any loft structure has approval.

## Final assessment

The Poiz Residences has **high defensive quality, good liquidity and moderate
appreciation potential**. Direct MRT and retail convenience, a low entry ticket
and strong observed rental activity make it easier to hold than many
non-integrated projects. Its historical matched-resale record is unusually
strong.

The evidence still does not support a project-wide claim of huge future
quantum. The best current conventional lead offers approximately S$50,000 of
price headroom against the latest same-size caveat. That is valuable buying
discipline, not a windfall. A S$300,000 five-year capital gain on that unit
requires an exit around S$2,707 psf and more than 5.5% annual appreciation,
well above the base underwriting range.

The practical strategy is to buy a verified seller—not a portal headline—at or
below the exact-layout buy box, favour efficient conventional strata area,
retain rental fallback and hold beyond SSD. At premium asks, expect ordinary
market-like compounding rather than a second launch-era rerating.
