## Decision

Arc at Tampines is an attractive **own-stay-plus-carry** proposition for a
price-sensitive upgrader, but the available evidence does not support
underwriting **huge capital appreciation** at most advertised prices. Its
strengths are functional family layouts, an upgrader-friendly entry quantum,
reasonable rental carry and a mature Tampines location. Its main valuation
constraint is enduring: the project is approximately 1.2 km from Tampines West
MRT and is not a station-integrated development.

The latest 12 complete months of caveats produced a median of approximately
**S$1,406.50 psf**, while 99.co reported a one-year average near
**S$1,390.56 psf**. The portal's displayed current asking-price average was
approximately S$1,499 psf, nearly 8% above its achieved-price average. That
gap, and several much larger unit-specific premiums, means entry discipline is
more important than the project's broad price story.

The best apparent opportunities in the captured inventory are:

1. **Best conventional investment entry:** the advertised 807 sqft two-bedroom
   at **S$1.095m**, or approximately S$1,357 psf. This is only about 1.9%
   above the latest same-size S$1.075m caveat and below the recent exact-size
   median. The advertisement's direct detail page has been inconsistent, so
   availability and a genuine executable seller price must be contact-tested.
2. **Best low-quantum family entry:** the SRX-only 958 sqft three-bedroom at
   **S$1.38m**, or approximately S$1,441 psf. It is close to recent exact-size
   achieved quanta of S$1.358m–S$1.385m. Treat it as a fair-value lead, not a
   proven discount, until the exact unit and live status are confirmed.
3. **Best normal-layout family value:** the SRX-only 1,130 sqft three-bedroom
   at **S$1.62m**, or approximately S$1,434 psf. If executable, it is almost
   exactly aligned with the April 2026 same-size S$1.625m caveat.
4. **Best four-bedroom lead:** the 1,292 sqft unit at **S$1.85m**. It is only
   about 3.9% above the latest same-size S$1.78m caveat, but approximately
   S$1.78m–S$1.82m is a better evidence-backed buy range.
5. **Special-situation own-stay lead:** the 1,679 sqft duplex penthouse
   advertised from **S$1.85m**. The low S$1,102 psf headline is driven by
   roof-terrace/duplex area and is not comparable with standard internal-area
   PSF. It may offer unusual lifestyle quantum, but only after a bank valuation
   and a measured internal-versus-outdoor area review.

Advertisements that appear materially expensive against like-for-like
caveats include the 807 sqft units at S$1.28m, the 958 sqft unit at S$1.60m,
the 1,130 sqft unit at S$1.899m and the 1,173 sqft four-bedroom at S$2.10m.
The last asks approximately S$1,790 psf, about 22% above the project's current
PSF record and roughly 27% above the latest same-size quantum.

Here, "quantum potential" means whether the current total purchase price leaves
credible resale and capital-appreciation headroom. If quantum means only the
absolute entry ticket, Arc's approximately S$1.10m two-bedroom and S$1.36m–
S$1.40m compact three-bedroom segments have broad upgrader appeal. Low absolute
quantum should not be confused with undervaluation.

## Scope and evidence rules

This report separates three evidence classes:

- **Advertised asking inventory:** evidence that an agent is marketing a unit,
  not proof that the unit is still available or that the seller will accept the
  displayed price.
- **Achieved caveats and rental contracts:** stronger market evidence, but
  still incomplete because renovation, condition, view, facing and every
  contractual term are not disclosed.
- **Scenario underwriting:** explicit analytical assumptions, not a price
  forecast or guaranteed return.

Primary achieved-price source:
[URA Property Market Information](https://eservice.ura.gov.sg/property-market-information/pmiResidentialTransactionSearch).
URA explains that resale and subsale records are based on voluntary caveats
lodged with SLA, are updated twice weekly and exclude transaction expenses from
the agreed price. Caveats are not exhaustive. See
[URA REALIS coverage and methodology](https://eservice.ura.gov.sg/reis/coverageandMethodology).

Rental contracts should be refreshed using
[URA's residential rental search](https://eservice.ura.gov.sg/property-market-information/pmiResidentialRentalSearch).
The search covers tenancy contracts submitted to IRAS for stamp-duty
assessment and reports floor area in bands rather than exact unit-level detail.

## Project and legal position

Arc at Tampines is a 574-unit executive condominium developed by a Hoi
Hup–Sunway joint venture. Its 99-year lease began on 22 February 2011 and it
obtained TOP on 5 August 2014, leaving approximately **84 years** on the lease
at the report date.

Official references:

- [HDB's sold EC-site record](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/cs/infoweb/-/media/doc/PLG/EC-Sites-Sold---14-Feb-2024.ashx);
- [HDB's EC TOP list](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/-/media/buying-a-flat/executive-condominiums/finding-an-ec/List-of-EC-Temporary-Occupation-Permit.pdf?hash=86A836CDCCF95F9A50892D832B9B63B4&sc_lang=en);
  and
- [EdgeProp's project record](https://www.edgeprop.sg/condo-apartment/arc-at-tampines).

The site was awarded for approximately S$187.59m, or S$302 psf per plot ratio.
The development completed its five-year minimum occupation period in August
2019 and its ten-year EC restriction period in August 2024. It is now treated
as a fully privatised condominium for resale eligibility. See
[HDB's conditions after buying an EC](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/sitecore/content/hdbinfoweb/home/buying-a-flat/executive-condominiums/conditions-after-buying-an-ec).

Full privatisation widens legal resale eligibility, but it is a **realised
catalyst**, not a future surprise. Almost two years of post-privatisation
transactions are already in the captured price evidence. Foreign ownership is
legally possible, but prevailing foreign-buyer ABSD materially limits the
practical size of that demand pool.

The rolling July 2025–June 2026 window contained 30 caveats, a median quantum
near S$1.39m and median PSF near S$1,406.50. That is approximately 5.2% of
the project's 574 units, or about 2.5 sales per month. Liquidity is healthy for
a family EC, but many individual layouts still trade only one to five times a
year.

The project launched in 2011 around S$740 psf:
[contemporary Arc launch and land-rate history](https://www.edgeprop.sg/property-news/government-releases-three-residential-sites-sale).
The complete new-sale record has a developer-sale median near S$738.50 psf,
versus the 2026 resale median near S$1,406.50 psf. That is approximately 90.5%
asset-price growth, or about **4.5% annualised** over roughly 14.5 years.

Historical matched-sale databases consequently show exceptional-looking
profitability:

- [PropertyNoob's profitability table](https://propertynoob.com/condo/arc-tampines/profitability)
  matched 283 profitable and zero unprofitable round trips in its captured
  snapshot;
- [OpenAgent](https://openagent.sg/property/arc-at-tampines) reported 294 out
  of 294 matched sales profitable, an average holding period near 8.8 years
  and an estimated floor premium around S$7 psf per floor; and
- the latest 12-month matched sample had a calculated median gross price gain
  near S$530k, median holding period near 10.45 years and median annualised
  asset-price return near 5.23%.

Aggregator counts differ because snapshots and matching methods differ. More
importantly, all reported profits are **gross asset-price changes**. They
exclude buyer's and additional buyer's stamp duty, legal and agency fees,
financing, maintenance, property tax, renovation, repairs and opportunity
cost.

A July 2026 buyer does not inherit the S$739–S$740 psf subsidised EC launch
base. The 100% historical gross-profit record is therefore not evidence that a
new buyer at approximately S$1,390–S$1,406 psf will reproduce old owners'
absolute gains.

OpenAgent's ownership snapshot estimates that 54.5% of observed units remain
with first owners, while 71.4% of recorded buyers came from HDB addresses.
This supports a broad upgrader market, but it also means:

- many remaining original owners have substantial embedded gains and room to
  sell below optimistic agent asks;
- demand is sensitive to total purchase quantum, financing and the timing of an
  HDB disposal; and
- an upgrader must not assume ABSD remission without satisfying the exact IRAS
  conditions.

build, while Pinery is integrated with Tampines West MRT and retail. New EC
buyers also accept eligibility rules and long sale restrictions in exchange
for subsidised pricing. Arc is older, has lease attrition and lacks direct MRT
access.

## Rental fallback

99.co reported average rent near **S$4.20 psf per month** over six months and
S$4.19 over one year. Dividing S$4.19 × 12 by the one-year sale average of
S$1,390.56 psf gives an indicative project gross yield of approximately
**3.62%**. EdgeProp independently displayed approximately 3.7%.

[PropertyNoob's project rental summary](https://propertynoob.com/condo/arc-tampines/)
reported:

| 3BR | 23 | S$4,348 | S$3,450–S$4,900 | 3.67% |
| 4BR | 4 | S$4,750 | S$4,200–S$5,000 | 3.26% |

Gross yield excludes maintenance, non-owner-occupier property tax, vacancy,
leasing commission, repairs, furnishing and financing. A S$1.80m dual-key
purchase needs aggregate rent of **S$6,000 per month** merely to show 4.0%
headline gross yield. Do not pay a dual-key premium without both tenancy
agreements, deposits, expiry dates and an expense-adjusted rent roll.

Rental medians stabilised below the 2023 peak rather than surging. Calculated
two-bedroom medians moved from approximately S$3,700 in 2023 to S$3,400 in
2025 and S$3,500 in 2026. Three-bedroom medians moved from approximately
S$4,500 to S$4,250 and about S$4,280. Composition changes prevent this from
being a same-unit rent index, but it does not support aggressive rent-growth
underwriting.

## Five-year capital-return test

The following illustration tests the strongest conventional lead: an S$1.095m
807 sqft purchase by a Singapore citizen buying a first residential property.

Assumptions:

- buyer's stamp duty: S$28,400;
- purchase legal cost: S$3,000;
- sale legal cost: S$3,000;
- selling commission: 2% plus 9% GST, or 2.18% of exit price;
- no ABSD;
- no mortgage interest, maintenance, property tax, vacancy or rental income;
  and
- sale after the SSD holding period.

Current duty references:
[IRAS buyer's stamp duty](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-property/buying-or-acquiring-property/buyer%27s-stamp-duty-%28bsd%29),
[IRAS ABSD](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-property/buying-or-acquiring-property/additional-buyer%27s-stamp-duty-%28absd%29)
and
[IRAS SSD](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-property/selling-or-disposing-property/seller%27s-stamp-duty-%28ssd%29-for-residential-property).

| Five-year price growth | Exit price | Exit PSF | Approx. capital-only profit/loss |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 0% p.a. | S$1.095m | S$1,357 | -S$58k |
| 2% p.a. | S$1.209m | S$1,498 | +S$53k |
| 3% p.a. | S$1.269m | S$1,573 | +S$112k |
| 4% p.a. | S$1.332m | S$1,651 | +S$174k |
| 5% p.a. | S$1.398m | S$1,732 | +S$238k |

The approximate capital-only break-even exit is **S$1.155m**, 5.4% above the
purchase price and about S$1,431 psf. Producing S$300,000 of capital-only
profit requires an exit near **S$1.461m**, or approximately **S$1,811 psf**.
That requires about 5.94% annual appreciation for five years and an exit PSF
roughly 23% above the current S$1,467 project record. A "huge" gain is
therefore a bull-plus outcome, not a responsible base case.

For a Singapore citizen buying a second residential property, a 20% ABSD adds
S$219,000 at entry. Under the same simplified assumptions, capital break-even
rises to approximately **S$1.378m**, or 25.9% above the purchase price. That
requires approximately 4.71% annual appreciation for five years merely to
recover transaction costs before holding costs. Even 5% annual price growth
produces only about S$19,000 of capital-only profit before those costs.

A married upgrader may qualify for ABSD remission only if all current
conditions are satisfied, including the required disposal timing:
[IRAS married-couple ABSD remission](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-property/appeals-refunds-reliefs-and-remissions/common-stamp-duty-remissions-and-reliefs-for-property/remission-of-absd-for-a-married-couple).
Buyer nationality, residency, ownership count and purchase structure must be
checked individually.

Purchases made on or after 4 July 2025 must generally be held for more than
four years to avoid SSD under the current rules. A five-year test leaves only a
narrow buffer for delays in completing a resale.

## Underwriting range

This is an analytical range, not a forecast:

| Scenario | Annual appreciation | Approx. five-year price change | Conditions |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Bear | -2% to 0% | -10% to 0% | OCR weakness, financing or employment shock, supply and ageing discount |
| Base | 1.5% to 3% | +8% to +16% | Mature-town demand, stable rents and ordinary market compounding |
| Bull | 4% to 5% | +22% to +28% | Strong wider market, new-launch umbrella and sustained eastern upgrader demand |

The historical approximately 4.5% annualised launch-to-current gain benefited
from a subsidised EC base, MOP release, full privatisation and the 2020–2025
market upswing. Median PSF grew approximately 15.7% from 2023 to 2025 but only
5.2% from 2024 to 2025 and was effectively flat in the partial 2026 sample.
This deceleration supports a 1.5%–3% base rather than simply extrapolating the
long-run launch return.

Paying 8%–20% above matching caveats effectively prepays much of the base or
bull case. Conversely, buying close to the latest exact-size caveat preserves
the possibility of ordinary compounding and makes rental carry more useful.

## Supporting fundamentals and catalysts

- **Mature Tampines demand:** Arc draws from a large established town with
  retail, healthcare, education, recreation and regional employment. This is a
  defensive demand base rather than an unproven estate.
- **Functional family stock:** the observed ownership mix is approximately
  25.3% two-bedroom, 57.6% three-bedroom and 17.1% four-bedroom. The S$1.1m–
  S$1.7m segments suit a broad upgrader pool.
- **Education and recreation:** Springfield Secondary is beside the
  development, Temasek Polytechnic is nearby, Bedok Reservoir Park provides
  formal recreation, and some stacks have open-space/quarry views. St Hilda's
  Primary is close to the 1 km boundary, but exact eligibility must be checked
  by block address using
  [MOE SchoolFinder](https://www.moe.gov.sg/schoolfinder).
- **Tampines Regional Centre rejuvenation:** URA plans continued improvement
  of Tampines' regional role, public spaces and active-mobility links.
  [URA East regional plan](https://www.ura.gov.sg/land-planning/master-plan/master-plan-2025/regional-plans/east/gateway-to-the-world--vibrant-nodes-for-all/).
- **Cross Island Line:** Phase 1 is targeted for 2030 and adds connectivity at
  Tampines North. Arc is not station-adjacent, so this is a broad town tailwind
  rather than a direct MRT re-rating trigger.
  [LTA Cross Island Line](https://www.lta.gov.sg/content/ltagov/en/upcoming_projects/rail_expansion/cross_island_line.html).
- **Eastern employment:** Changi Terminal 5 is a long-duration employment and
  infrastructure tailwind for the east, with delivery targeted for the
  mid-2030s.
  [Changi Airport Terminal 5](https://www.changiairport.com/en/corporate/about-us/future-developments/terminal-5.html).
- **Paya Lebar Air Base:** relocation from the 2030s may eventually create a
  major mixed-use district and connections west of Tampines. The horizon is
  remote and new housing supply is a counterweight.
  [URA Paya Lebar Air Base plans](https://www.ura.gov.sg/land-planning/master-plan/master-plan-2025/regional-plans/east/paya-lebar-air-base/).

These are broad regional tailwinds. None is a clearly unpriced, Arc-specific
catalyst comparable with a new station at the project's gate.

## Risks and limits to upside

- **No doorstep MRT:** 99.co estimates Tampines West MRT at approximately
  1,210 m or a 16-minute walk. Arc remains feeder-bus dependent, which explains
  part of the persistent discount to Trilliant, CityLife, Rivelle and Pinery.
- **Privatisation is already priced:** the August 2024 eligibility expansion
  has already passed and is represented in 2025–2026 transactions.
- **Age and lease at exit:** a buyer exiting in five to eight years will market
  a roughly 17–20-year-old project with about 76–79 lease years remaining.
  Maintenance and age differentiation will matter more than at Rivelle,
  Pinery, Tapestry or Parc Central.
- **Nearby substitution:** Arc, Q Bay, Santorini, Tapestry and Parc Central
  together provide approximately 3,362 existing units in the broader enclave.
  Family buyers have many close substitutes.
- **Parc Central supply:** the 700-unit Parc Central reaches MOP around 2029,
  adding relatively new EC resale stock close to a plausible Arc exit window.
  [EdgeProp Parc Central record](https://www.edgeprop.sg/condo-apartment/parc-central-residences).
- **Original-owner selling headroom:** more than half of observed units remain
  with first owners who bought cheaply. This can support transactions but also
  creates sellers able to undercut aspirational asks while retaining large
  profits.
- **Misleading low PSF:** patio, PES, roof-terrace, penthouse and dual-key
  layouts can display low PSF because outdoor or split-use area is included.
  Absolute quantum, liveable internal area and future buyer depth matter more.
- **Unit-specific exposure:** school activity, Tampines Avenue traffic,
  afternoon sun, low-floor privacy, pool noise and future view changes vary by
  block and stack.
- **Soft current OCR evidence:** URA reported OCR non-landed prices down 0.1%
  quarter on quarter and rents down 0.3% in Q2 2026. OCR vacancy rose to 5.6%.
  [URA Q2 2026 statistics](https://www.ura.gov.sg/news/media/pr26-57/).
- **National supply:** URA reported approximately 60,600 private homes,
  including ECs, expected to complete in coming years. New regional amenities
  and infrastructure can introduce supply as well as demand.
- **Financing and tax:** a high-LTV or ABSD-paying buyer can have negative
  capital economics despite a rising asset price. Gross yield does not equal
  positive cash flow.

## Due diligence before an option to purchase

For any shortlisted unit, obtain:

1. The exact block, unit number, floor, stack and written seller-authorised
   reserve or executable price. Confirm that the advertisement remains live.
2. A bank valuation and exact stack/floor caveats rather than project-wide PSF
   averages alone.
3. A measured floor-plan review separating internal liveable area from PES,
   patio, void, staircase and roof-terrace area. This is mandatory for the
   1,356, 1,421 and 1,679 sqft layouts.
4. Daytime, school-dismissal, evening and wet-weather inspections for road,
   school and pool noise, west sun, privacy, water ingress and feeder-bus
   convenience.
5. MCST maintenance and sinking-fund amounts, AGM minutes, special levies,
   lift and facade works, waterproofing history, defects and renovation
   approvals.
6. If tenanted, the full tenancy agreement, deposit transfer, rent receipts,
   expiry, diplomatic and break clauses, tenant history, inventory and any
   below-market or related-party rent.
7. A complete expense model including maintenance, property tax, vacancy,
   leasing commission, repairs, furnishing, insurance and financing.
8. Buyer-specific BSD, ABSD, possible married-couple remission, SSD timeline,
   total debt servicing ratio, CPF usage and cash-flow buffer.
9. Exact primary-school distance and registration eligibility by the
   individual block address; do not rely on a general project advertisement.
10. Planning checks for future obstruction or construction exposure and a
    comparison of the chosen stack with nearby Q Bay, Santorini, Tapestry and
    Parc Central alternatives at the same total quantum.

For the S$1.095m 807 sqft lead specifically, verify the advertised reduction,
vacant/occupied status, enclosed-kitchen alterations, floor and facing. A
headline discount can be erased by a low bank valuation, road exposure,
tenancy constraint or unauthorised works.

## Final assessment

Arc at Tampines has **moderate, price-dependent appreciation potential** and a
respectable rental fallback. It does not have high-confidence huge quantum
upside from July 2026 resale values. Its broad project discount is largely
explained by age and lack of direct MRT access, while the August 2024
privatisation catalyst is already realised.

The S$1.095m 807 sqft, S$1.38m 958 sqft and S$1.62m 1,130 sqft advertisements
are worth immediate contact-testing because they sit close to achieved value
and preserve a broad future buyer quantum. None shows a clean 10%–20%
like-for-like discount. Their likely advantage is disciplined entry plus
ordinary market compounding, not an instant valuation windfall.

For a first-property Singapore-citizen buyer at S$1.095m, a five-year capital
profit near S$300k requires an exit around S$1.461m or S$1,811 psf—about 23%
above the project's present record and nearly 5.94% annual appreciation. That
is possible only in a strong bull case. For a buyer incurring 20% ABSD, even
5% annual growth barely clears simplified transaction costs before holding
costs.

The strongest use case is therefore a household that values the space and
Tampines location, can accept feeder-bus dependence, plans a sufficiently long
hold and buys at or below an exact-size caveat-supported ceiling. A pure
capital-appreciation investor should demand a larger entry discount or prefer
a project with a credible unpriced transport catalyst.
