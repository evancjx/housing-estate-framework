## Decision

**Canberra Crescent Residences is not a blanket huge-potential-quantum buy at the prices captured.** It is an uncompleted 376-unit private condominium, not an Executive Condominium (EC). The URA-source ledger contains 340 New Sales through July 2026 and no Sub Sale or Resale. Those sales reconcile exactly to a July public balance chart implying 36 larger homes remained; a newer 13 August agent chart reports 342 sold and 34 available. The two additional marketing-reported sales await official-source corroboration. No public feed is treated as contractual inventory, and no owner exit yet proves secondary-market liquidity near S$2,000 psf.

The project did implement an observable early-Q4 price step. Across all **95 exact units** with a current price in both the 13 September and 12 November 2025 Huttons schedules, prices increased by a mean **S$21,688 / 1.008%**, or approximately S$20 psf. The transaction sequence brackets use of the new tier to **2–5 November 2025**. The price-list mechanics are consistent with withdrawal of an initial roughly 1% promotion; no cited developer announcement establishes the commercial rationale.

The nearest modern completed substitutes are materially closer to Canberra MRT and cheaper. In the same August 2025–July 2026 window, matched The Watergardens at Canberra and The Commodore cohorts generally achieved about S$1,637–S$1,789 psf. The current Canberra Crescent starting points are about S$1,947–S$2,006 psf. The premium can partly pay for a newer lease, specifications and future delivery, but it is **not** supported by better rail access or a demonstrated rental stream.

For an August 2025 three-bedroom buyer considering a September 2026 disposal, a 3% gross price CAGR is arithmetically possible but not an investable realised return. The representative 990 sq ft launch entry of S$1.993m needs about S$2.058m merely for 3% gross price growth, but approximately **S$2.497m** to retain 3% after original BSD, a S$5,000 acquisition allowance, 2.5% selling friction and 12% SSD. Selling at the gross-growth target still produces about **-S$308k** before finance and holding costs. Waiting beyond the exact SSD period is materially more coherent than a quick exit.

The most defensible screens are:

1. **Best lower-quantum/liquidity screen:** 990 sq ft 3BR Premium at about **S$1.986m / S$2,006 psf**. It is 0.9% below the same-size achieved median quantum, but completed 958–1,012 sq ft alternatives are about S$0.25m–S$0.39m cheaper and nearer the station.
2. **Best arithmetic value if the physical trade-off is accepted:** 1,216 sq ft 4BR Standard PES at about **S$2.367m / S$1,947 psf**. It is 1.5% below its exact-size achieved median and produces the strongest illustrated profit at a common future PSF. A ground-floor/PES discount can persist because of privacy, moisture, pest, road and facility-noise risks.
3. **Fairer compact-four-bedroom screen:** 1,173 sq ft at about **S$2.324m / S$1,981 psf**, only 0.5% above its own achieved median quantum. Confirm whether the apparent remaining unit is PES and whether usable internal area is acceptable.
4. **Avoid the 1,163 sq ft floor price captured on 3 August without a special reason:** S$2.297m is about 4.1% above its exact-size cohort median and above the captured achieved maximum.
5. **Bull-case absolute-upside format only:** 1,324 sq ft 4BR Premium at about **S$2.654m**. Its larger capital base makes a nominal S$500,000 hurdle require a lower growth rate, but six of only twelve such homes appear unsold and the required S$3.345m exit has no demonstrated District 27 buyer pool.

Under a Singapore-citizen first-property, no-ABSD, capital-only convention, an S$2,300 psf future sale produces approximately **S$160,000–S$267,000** for the 990–1,324 sq ft captured entry tickets before finance and holding costs. To make S$500,000, the required exits are about **S$2,497–S$2,652 psf** and S$2.626m–S$3.345m. Those PSFs are 16%–23% above the project's captured record of S$2,151 psf. That is possible only as a long-horizon bull case, not current transaction evidence.

## Evidence boundary and source semantics

This is a **property analysis**, not a factor-audit report and not a unified property ranking. It tests whether the captured purchase prices leave credible room for resale and absolute-dollar appreciation. Provision and person-relative Liveability remain separate in the wider framework.

The report separates six evidence classes:

- **Official project disclosure:** tenure, expected-vacant-possession date, unit mix and floor plans disclosed in the official brochure.
- **Contractual control:** the exact executed Sale and Purchase Agreement and its schedules; brochure content is not treated as a contractual promise.
- **Achieved transaction evidence:** lodged URA/SLA caveats. Caveats are voluntary and are not an exhaustive record; New Sale, Sub Sale and Resale represent different asset states.
- **Dated offered-price schedules:** time-stamped asking schedules for exact floor-stack units; useful for like-for-like repricing, but not proof of an exercised sale, net incentive or developer rationale.
- **Public balance and asking evidence:** useful for finding possible stock and seller intent, but not proof that a unit is available, unique or reservable at the headline price.
- **Scenario underwriting:** explicit calculations, not a valuation, forecast or guaranteed return.

### Why the raw median suggests a false 23% jump

The monthly median transaction quantum rose from **S$1.610 million in October to S$1.986 million in November**, an apparent **23.4%** increase. That is not a like-for-like project price increase. October contained 20 sales of 797 sqft units and three of 667 sqft units; November contained only four 797 sqft sales, no 667 sqft sales, and seven of its 13 sales were 990 sqft or larger. The median moved to a more expensive layout cohort.

The continuous-unit comparison controls for this composition change by following the same 95 exact floor-stack units. Its **1.008%** result is the relevant estimate of the schedule change; the 23.4% quantum movement is a mix artefact.

### Three-percent annualised-return test

The gross-price test asks only what sale price makes the apartment price compound by 3% for 13 months:

`gross target = purchase price × 1.03^(13/12)`

The cost-adjusted test compounds acquisition basis and requires proceeds after SSD and sale friction to equal that future value:

`cost-adjusted target = (purchase price + original BSD + S$5,000) × 1.03^(13/12) ÷ (1 − 2.5% − 12%)`

## Capital-potential model

### Common convention

The model assumes:

- a Singapore citizen buying a first residential property, with **zero ABSD**;
- captured lowest public entry price, BSD rates at the 13 August capture and S$5,000 legal/technical allowance;
- net resale proceeds equal to 97.5% of gross sale price, allowing 2.5% selling, GST/legal and execution friction;
- resale after the four-year SSD period; and
- no mortgage interest, progressive-payment carry, maintenance, property tax, insurance, renovation, vacancy, rental income or opportunity cost.

Thus `capital-only profit = 0.975 × gross exit − purchase price − BSD − S$5,000`.

This is intentionally generous to potential quantum. A true all-in profit will be lower when financing and holding costs are positive.

## Tax, quick-sale and HDB-owner effects

[IRAS SSD rules](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-property/selling-or-disposing-property/seller%27s-stamp-duty-%28ssd%29-for-residential-property) apply a four-year holding schedule to residential properties bought from 4 July 2025: 16%, 12%, 8% and 4% for disposal in the first through fourth holding years. A buyer purchasing at the 13 August 2026 capture should therefore plan around the exact 2030 acquisition anniversary, not an approximate calendar year.

For context, an August 2025 launch buyer selling in September 2026 would generally still be in the 12% SSD band, subject to exact dates. Using launch-period format medians, 2.5% exit friction, original BSD and S$5,000 allowance, rough SSD-inclusive break-even is about S$2,442 psf for 990 sq ft and S$2,393–S$2,394 psf for 1,216/1,324 sq ft. There is no current achieved owner-exit evidence near those levels, so a near-term flip is not credible.

For a Singapore citizen buying a second residential property without remission, the **20% ABSD rate at capture** changes the thesis materially:

| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 990 sq ft | S$397,192 | S$2.520m / S$2,546 psf | S$3.033m / S$3,064 psf |

ABSD is a cash/tax cost, not equity in the property. It subtracts dollar-for-dollar from every illustrated no-ABSD profit before financing.

Canberra Crescent itself has no EC eligibility test and no EC MOP of either policy vintage. Retaining the flat can also make the purchase ABSD-liable. Buyer profile, citizenship, existing-home disposal timing, remission and CPF use must be checked with a conveyancing solicitor and tax adviser before an option is issued.

## Rental fallback

There are no achieved project rents because Canberra Crescent is uncompleted. Rent at 2030 delivery cannot be assumed from the October 2025–June 2026 peer-rent window, and 376 concurrent handovers can create temporary landlord competition.

Recent completed-peer evidence provides only a proxy. The reproducible displayed-contract sample runs from **October 2025 through June 2026**. ## Key risks

- **No secondary proof:** zero project Sub Sales and Resales means entry-to-exit spread and owner liquidity are unknown.
- **Residual-mix risk:** smaller formats sold first; all apparent remaining stock is 990 sq ft or larger.
- **Premium risk:** nearby modern completed substitutes are 9%–20% cheaper on matched PSF and have better station access.
- **Developer-stock competition:** an owner selling before stock clears may compete with developer incentives and fresh contracts.
- **Delivery/carry risk:** expected vacant possession is April 2030; progressive payments and interest accrue before rent.
- **PES/ground-floor risk:** the lowest 1,163/1,173/1,216/1,324 sq ft prices appear tied to PES variants whose discount can persist.
- **Large-ticket liquidity:** S$3m-plus District 27 exits have a narrower owner-occupier pool than sub-S$2m stock.
- **School-distance risk:** official category is block/address-specific, can be recalculated and never guarantees a P1 place.
- **Planning-timeline risk:** shipyard and uncommitted rail ideas can take many years or change; no speculative premium is underwritten.
- **Supply and policy risk:** private GLS and EC supply, ABSD, SSD, financing rules and interest rates can change demand.
- **Public-feed risk:** even mutually consistent balance, portal and price-from pages can lag reservations, cancellations or option exercises.

## Buyer verification checklist

Before paying any booking fee or signing an Option to Purchase:

1. Obtain the developer's **same-day written balance**, exact unit number, net price, promotion, expiry and reservation status.
2. Reconcile block, stack, floor, facing, ceiling height, strata area and official floor-plan type to the Sale and Purchase Agreement.
3. For PES units, inspect privacy, drainage, waterproofing, pest exposure, facility/road noise, enclosure rules and actual internal usable area.
4. Walk the real route to Canberra MRT and bus stops at commute time; do not rely on straight-line or an incorrect legacy coordinate.
5. Check afternoon sun, expressway/industrial/air-noise exposure, future construction plots and views from the exact stack.
6. Re-run OneMap SchoolQuery using the exact block/postal address for the applicable P1 exercise and read MOE distance rules.
7. Build a progressive-payment cash-flow model with loan stress, buyer stamp duties, CPF effects and zero rent until a conservative completion date.
8. Have a solicitor confirm citizenship profile, BSD, ABSD/remission, HDB MOP, SSD dates, ownership form and all contract disclosures.
9. Obtain current bank valuations and compare the exact unit with achieved same-area/floor evidence—not a project-average record.
10. Stress an exit at S$2,000–S$2,100 psf, six months' vacancy and lower rent; reject the purchase if that outcome impairs household finances.
11. Treat agent balance sites, portal cards, rebates and future-planning claims as leads until documentary evidence is supplied.
12. Decide the intended exit buyer in advance: upgrader family, own-stayer or investor, and verify that the future total quantum is affordable to that pool.

## Bottom line

Canberra Crescent Residences launched strongly: 150 homes sold on opening weekend, 277 URA New Sales were recorded by end-October before the observed 2–5 November repricing window, and the official-source snapshot reached 340 / 376 or 90.4% through July 2026. A newer marketing chart reports 342 / 376 or 91.0%, but its two additional sales and 34-unit balance remain pending official-source corroboration.

The Q4 price finding is precise but modest. For all 95 units continuously priced between the 13 September and 12 November schedules, the former listed price became the new current price. The mean change was **S$21,688 / 1.008%**, approximately S$20 psf, and the evidence brackets the new tier to 2–5 November 2025. The 23.4% October-to-November median-quantum jump was a change in sold-unit mix—not a 23% developer increase. Sales slowed afterward, but the data cannot attribute that slowdown to the 1% revision alone.

The project retains a credible own-stay proposition: a new private condominium with full facilities, nearby bus access, Canberra Plaza, Bukit Canberra and at least one 3 August OneMap primary-school-within-1km category for every block. The land basis and strong sell-through are project positives.

They do not establish huge quantum for a buyer using these captures. The 3 August asking evidence was roughly S$1,950–S$2,006 psf for residual homes, while completed, closer-to-MRT modern substitutes transacted around S$1,637–S$1,789 psf in the reviewed window. There is no achieved subject resale or rent, all smaller formats have been absorbed, and the reported S$2m–S$2.65m residual stock must eventually exit into a relatively narrow buyer pool.

For an existing August 2025 three-bedroom buyer, a 2026 owner exit is a different and weaker proposition. The 990 sqft ordinary working range of S$1.95m–S$2.05m does not overcome 12% SSD and stated friction. Even the S$2.058m gross 3%-CAGR target leaves approximately -S$308k under the cost model; retaining 3% requires S$2.497m / S$2,522 psf. An SSD-free September 2029 scenario reduces the comparable cost-adjusted target to S$2.392m / S$2,416 psf, though it remains a scenario rather than a forecast.

For a new Singapore-citizen first-property buyer with a long horizon, the **990 sq ft** is the safer liquidity choice and the **1,216 sq ft PES** is the better arithmetic choice if its physical disadvantages are acceptable. A sensible base case is a small capital-only loss/break-even to moderate S$0.1m–S$0.27m upside around S$2,100–S$2,300 psf—not S$500,000. The latter requires S$2,497–S$2,652 psf, strong multi-year appreciation and successful execution before financing and holding costs.

The project is therefore classified **moderate/selective potential quantum, not high-confidence huge potential quantum**. Do not proceed without an exact unit, same-day contractual balance and stack-specific due diligence.
