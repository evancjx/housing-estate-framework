## Decision

Stratford Court is a **selective value buy for space**, not a
high-confidence huge-quantum investment. Its low-rise character, 268-unit
scale and 980–2,000 sqft legacy floor plates provide family utility near Tanah
Merah MRT. The trade-off is a 1995 lease, ageing roofs and facilities, and
limited evidence that its larger absolute-quantum units will rerate at the
same PSF as compact newer projects.

The strongest ordinary family buy box is approximately **1,152–1,227 sqft at
S$1.38m–S$1.45m**. Recent 1,152–1,163 sqft resales achieved S$1.40m–S$1.45m,
while live asking evidence spans roughly S$1.40m to S$1.589m. The lower end is
supported; the top end needs a superior layout, private terrace, floor,
renovation or facing.

The conditional entry ladder is:

1. **At or below S$1.40m:** attractive for a normal 1,152–1,227 sqft unit
   after condition and MCST checks.
2. **S$1.41m–S$1.47m:** fair and supported by current achieved evidence.
3. **S$1.48m–S$1.55m:** requires a meaningful unit-specific advantage.
4. **Above S$1.55m:** premium territory for the ordinary family segment.

Large 1,600–2,034 sqft units form a separate market. Their recent achieved PSF
is often lower because total quantum and upkeep narrow the replacement buyer
pool. A low headline PSF is not an arbitrage.

At a S$1.40m representative entry, eight-year 3% annual growth produces about
**S$284k capital-only profit** before financing and holding costs. A S$1m
capital-only profit needs an exit around **S$2.508m**. That is a bull case for
an ordinary 1,160 sqft unit. Potential quantum is **moderate, not huge**.

## Scope and evidence rules

This is a property analysis, not a factor-audit report or unified ranking. It
separates raw advertised asks from achieved caveats and scenario assumptions.
Portal cards can duplicate one unit; caveats do not reveal full condition,
facing or incentives; scenarios are not forecasts.

The achieved-price reference is
[URA Property Market Information](https://eservice.ura.gov.sg/property-market-information/pmiResidentialTransactionSearch).
Caveats are voluntarily lodged and not exhaustive. Exact strata area, floor
plan, private terrace or garden area, block, floor and stack are better
comparables than portal bedroom labels.

## Project proposition

[GuocoLand's official project page](https://www.guocoland.com.sg/SG/Stratford-Court.asp)
states 268 homes, completion in 1998 and an English Regent low-rise design with
sloping roofs, large windows, open balconies and private garden terraces. It
describes Tanah Merah MRT as a short walk.

The captured URA records state a 99-year lease commencing in June 1995,
leaving approximately **67–68 years**. Around 59–60 years would remain after
an eight-year hold. That is not an immediate cliff, but exit liquidity should
be underwritten rather than assumed.

The low-rise proposition gives privacy, light and usable space. It also creates
more roof, façade, balcony and waterproofing surface per home than a simple
modern tower. Those capital items belong in the investment decision.

## Underwriting and future quantum

The representative case is an ordinary approximately 1,152–1,163 sqft family
unit at **S$1.40m**. BSD is **S$40,600** under current
[IRAS residential BSD tiers](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-property/buying-or-acquiring-property/buyer%27s-stamp-duty-%28bsd%29).
The illustration assumes dutiable value equals entry price; actual BSD and
ABSD apply to the higher of purchase consideration or market value.
The no-ABSD case assumes a Singapore citizen buying a first residential
property.

`capital-only profit = exit price × 0.975 − entry price − BSD − S$5,000 acquisition legal/technical allowance`

The assumed 2.5% selling friction and fixed S$5,000 acquisition allowance are
included. Scenarios exclude any other legal fees, finance, maintenance,
renovation, property tax, rent, vacancy and opportunity cost. The
second-property sensitivity also deducts **20% of entry** under the current
[SC second-property ABSD rate](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-property/buying-or-acquiring-property/additional-buyer%27s-stamp-duty-%28absd%29).

| Hold / annual price growth | Exit quantum | SC first-property capital-only profit | SC second-property after 20% ABSD |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 years / 0% | S$1.400m | -S$81k | -S$361k |
| 5 years / 2% | S$1.546m | S$61k | -S$219k |
| 5 years / 3% | S$1.623m | S$137k | -S$143k |
| 5 years / 4% | S$1.703m | S$215k | -S$65k |
| 8 years / 0% | S$1.400m | -S$81k | -S$361k |
| 8 years / 2% | S$1.640m | S$154k | -S$126k |
| 8 years / 3% | S$1.773m | S$284k | S$4k |
| 8 years / 4% | S$1.916m | S$422k | S$142k |

Capital-only break-even is about 1.2% annually over five years or 0.7% over
eight before holding costs. Current
[SSD rules](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-property/selling-or-disposing-property/seller%27s-stamp-duty-%28ssd%29-for-residential-property)
apply for four years to purchases from 4 July 2025; the modelled exits avoid
SSD if dates and rules are unchanged.

## Catalysts

- Large usable floor plates at a lower total quantum than newer family units.
- Low-rise character and private garden/terrace formats can command an own-stay
  premium.
- Existing East-West Line connectivity and station-area retail improvement.
- A purchase near S$1.40m does not require project-record PSF to exit.

## Risks

- Approximately 67–68 lease years remain now and 59–60 after eight years.
- Sloping roofs, balconies, terraces, façades and waterproofing create
  project-specific capital-work exposure.
- Low PSF on large units is partly a total-quantum and liquidity discount.
- Newer projects can cap rerating even if their rooms are smaller.
- Nine annual caveats are useful but not deep; special layouts are less liquid.
- No current collective-sale tender was verified. En-bloc optionality is zero
  in the base case.

## Due diligence before an offer

1. Obtain title, exact strata and private-area split, floor plan, lease dates,
   share value and renovation approvals.
2. Review three years of MCST accounts and AGM minutes, sinking fund, roof and
   façade programmes, water-ingress claims, insurance, major works and special
   levies.
3. Inspect rooflines, terraces, balcony thresholds, plumbing, windows, lifts,
   carpark and noise.
4. Verify tenancy and vacant possession.
5. Obtain bank valuation and compare only same-size, similar-layout caveats.

## Final assessment

Stratford Court is best approached as a **space-for-quantum own-stay or
rental-fallback purchase around S$1.38m–S$1.45m for 1,152–1,227 sqft**. The
project can deliver moderate capital growth if bought well and maintained. It
does not support a huge-quantum thesis, and paying above S$1.55m for an ordinary
unit removes most of the evidence-backed entry advantage.
