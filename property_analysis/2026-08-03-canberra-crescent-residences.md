## Scope and evidence rules

This is a **property analysis**, not a factor-audit report and not a unified property ranking. It tests whether today's total purchase price leaves credible room for resale and absolute-dollar appreciation. Provision and person-relative Liveability remain separate in the wider framework.

The report separates five evidence classes:

- **Official project disclosure:** tenure, expected-vacant-possession date, unit mix and floor plans disclosed in the official brochure.
- **Contractual control:** the exact executed Sale and Purchase Agreement and its schedules; brochure content is not treated as a contractual promise.
- **Achieved transaction evidence:** lodged URA/SLA caveats. Caveats are voluntary and are not an exhaustive record; New Sale, Sub Sale and Resale represent different asset states.
- **Public balance and asking evidence:** useful for finding possible stock and seller intent, but not proof that a unit is available, unique or reservable at the headline price.
- **Scenario underwriting:** explicit calculations, not a valuation, forecast or guaranteed return.

HDB, EC and ordinary private-condominium evidence is **not pooled into a single price ranking**. Northern EC sites may affect the eligible upgrader pool, but their income ceiling, eligibility and initial-ownership restrictions make them a different tenure/product universe. [HDB's current EC conditions](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/buying-a-flat/executive-condominiums/conditions-after-buying-an-ec) state that projects whose land-sale tender closed on or after 8 May 2026 can be sold on the open market only after a ten-year MOP; older-tender sites retain the earlier five-year EC restriction. The six-site pipeline below therefore contains both policy vintages.

The primary achieved-price source is [URA Property Market Information](https://eservice.ura.gov.sg/property-market-information/pmiResidentialTransactionSearch). Raw calculations use the repository's D27 URA-source export through July 2026 because it is later than the detailed EdgeProp export, which ends on 15 June 2026. Bedroom and layout matches are assigned only after reconciling exact areas to the official brochure; URA does not publish bedrooms.

## Capital-potential model

### Common convention

The model assumes:

- a Singapore citizen buying a first residential property, with **zero ABSD**;
- captured lowest public entry price, current BSD and S$5,000 legal/technical allowance;
- net resale proceeds equal to 97.5% of gross sale price, allowing 2.5% selling, GST/legal and execution friction;
- resale after the four-year SSD period; and
- no mortgage interest, progressive-payment carry, maintenance, property tax, insurance, renovation, vacancy, rental income or opportunity cost.

Thus `capital-only profit = 0.975 × gross exit − purchase price − BSD − S$5,000`.

This is intentionally generous to potential quantum. A true all-in profit will be lower when financing and holding costs are positive.

## Tax, quick-sale and HDB-owner effects

[IRAS SSD rules](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-property/selling-or-disposing-property/seller%27s-stamp-duty-%28ssd%29-for-residential-property) apply a four-year holding schedule to residential properties bought from 4 July 2025: 16%, 12%, 8% and 4% for disposal in the first through fourth holding years. A current buyer should therefore plan around the exact 2030 acquisition anniversary, not an approximate calendar year.

For context, an August 2025 launch buyer selling in September 2026 would generally still be in the 12% SSD band, subject to exact dates. Using launch-period format medians, 2.5% exit friction, original BSD and S$5,000 allowance, rough SSD-inclusive break-even is about S$2,442 psf for 990 sq ft and S$2,393–S$2,394 psf for 1,216/1,324 sq ft. There is no current achieved owner-exit evidence near those levels, so a near-term flip is not credible.

For a Singapore citizen buying a second residential property without remission, current **20% ABSD** changes the thesis materially:

| Format | Additional 20% ABSD | Capital-only break-even including ABSD | Exit for S$500k including ABSD |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 990 sq ft | S$397,192 | S$2.520m / S$2,546 psf | S$3.033m / S$3,064 psf |
| 1,216 sq ft | S$473,407 | S$3.009m / S$2,474 psf | S$3.521m / S$2,896 psf |
| 1,324 sq ft | S$530,798 | S$3.377m / S$2,550 psf | S$3.889m / S$2,938 psf |

ABSD is a cash/tax cost, not equity in the property. It subtracts dollar-for-dollar from every illustrated no-ABSD profit before financing.

Canberra Crescent itself has no EC eligibility test and no EC MOP of either policy vintage. An HDB owner is still subject to [HDB rules on acquiring private residential property](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/managing-my-home/home-ownership/acquiring-private-property), including fulfilling the applicable HDB MOP. Retaining the flat can also make the purchase ABSD-liable. Buyer profile, citizenship, existing-home disposal timing, remission and CPF use must be checked with a conveyancing solicitor and tax adviser before an option is issued.

## Rental fallback

There are no achieved project rents because Canberra Crescent is uncompleted. Rent at 2030 delivery cannot be assumed from today's cycle, and 376 concurrent handovers can create temporary landlord competition.

Recent completed-peer evidence provides only a proxy. The reproducible displayed-contract sample runs from **October 2025 through June 2026**. It retains bedroom count and the public 100 sq ft rent band rather than pretending the portal publishes an exact floor plan. For 990 sq ft, the sample uses all six relevant observations visible in the latest peer tables: five Watergardens 3BR contracts in the 900–1,100 sq ft bands in June 2026 and one Commodore 3BR 900–1,000 sq ft contract in May 2026. Separately, the awarded Chencharu Close mixed-use site can yield about **875 ordinary private residential flats** near Khatib MRT. This is a large direct District 27 private-housing competitor and cannot be hidden inside the EC total.

The macro backdrop is not a guaranteed rising tide. [URA's Q2 2026 release](https://www.ura.gov.sg/news/media/pr26-57/) reported:

- OCR non-landed rents down 0.3%;
- OCR completed-private vacancy at 5.6%, up from 5.2%; and
- about 60,600 private homes including ECs expected to complete over the coming years.

This supports prudence around a S$2m–S$2.65m uncompleted northern entry.

## Key risks

- **No secondary proof:** zero project Sub Sales and Resales means entry-to-exit spread and owner liquidity are unknown.
- **Residual-mix risk:** smaller formats sold first; all apparent remaining stock is 990 sq ft or larger.
- **Premium risk:** nearby modern completed substitutes are 9%–20% cheaper on matched PSF and have better station access.
- **Developer-stock competition:** an owner selling before stock clears may compete with developer incentives and fresh contracts.
- **Delivery/carry risk:** expected vacant possession is April 2030; progressive payments and interest accrue before rent.
- **PES/ground-floor risk:** the lowest 1,163/1,173/1,216/1,324 sq ft prices appear tied to PES variants whose discount can persist.
- **Large-ticket liquidity:** S$3m-plus District 27 exits have a narrower owner-occupier pool than sub-S$2m stock.
- **School-distance risk:** official category is block/address-specific, can be recalculated and never guarantees a P1 place.
- **Planning-timeline risk:** shipyard and uncommitted rail ideas can take many years or change; no speculative premium is underwritten.
- **Supply and policy risk:** private GLS and EC supply, ABSD, SSD, financing rules and interest rates can change demand.
- **Public-feed risk:** even mutually consistent balance, portal and price-from pages can lag reservations, cancellations or option exercises.

## Buyer verification checklist

Before paying any booking fee or signing an Option to Purchase:

1. Obtain the developer's **same-day written balance**, exact unit number, net price, promotion, expiry and reservation status.
2. Reconcile block, stack, floor, facing, ceiling height, strata area and official floor-plan type to the Sale and Purchase Agreement.
3. For PES units, inspect privacy, drainage, waterproofing, pest exposure, facility/road noise, enclosure rules and actual internal usable area.
4. Walk the real route to Canberra MRT and bus stops at commute time; do not rely on straight-line or an incorrect legacy coordinate.
5. Check afternoon sun, expressway/industrial/air-noise exposure, future construction plots and views from the exact stack.
6. Re-run OneMap SchoolQuery using the exact block/postal address for the applicable P1 exercise and read MOE distance rules.
7. Build a progressive-payment cash-flow model with loan stress, buyer stamp duties, CPF effects and zero rent until a conservative completion date.
8. Have a solicitor confirm citizenship profile, BSD, ABSD/remission, HDB MOP, SSD dates, ownership form and all contract disclosures.
9. Obtain current bank valuations and compare the exact unit with achieved same-area/floor evidence—not a project-average record.
10. Stress an exit at S$2,000–S$2,100 psf, six months' vacancy and lower rent; reject the purchase if that outcome impairs household finances.
11. Treat agent balance sites, portal cards, rebates and future-planning claims as leads until documentary evidence is supplied.
12. Decide the intended exit buyer in advance: upgrader family, own-stayer or investor, and verify that the future total quantum is affordable to that pool.

## Conclusion

Canberra Crescent Residences has a credible own-stay proposition: new project, full facilities, nearby bus access, Canberra Plaza, Bukit Canberra and at least one current OneMap primary-school-within-1km category for every block. The land basis and strong 90.4% caveat reconciliation are developer/project-quality positives.

They do not establish huge quantum for today's buyer. The project already asks roughly S$1,950–S$2,006 psf for residual homes while completed, closer-to-MRT modern substitutes transact around S$1,637–S$1,789 psf. There is no achieved subject resale or rent, all smaller formats have been absorbed, and the remaining S$2m–S$2.65m stock must eventually exit into a relatively narrow buyer pool.

For a Singapore-citizen first-property buyer with a long horizon, the **990 sq ft** is the safer liquidity choice and the **1,216 sq ft PES** is the better arithmetic choice if its physical disadvantages are acceptable. A sensible base case is a small capital-only loss/break-even to moderate S$0.1m–S$0.27m upside around S$2,100–S$2,300 psf—not S$500,000. The latter requires S$2,497–S$2,652 psf, strong multi-year appreciation and successful execution before financing and holding costs.

The project is therefore classified **moderate/selective potential quantum, not high-confidence huge potential quantum**. Do not proceed without an exact unit, same-day contractual balance and stack-specific due diligence.
