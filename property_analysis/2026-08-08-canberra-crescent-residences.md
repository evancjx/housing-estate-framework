## Decision

**A Canberra Crescent Residences three-bedroom home is unlikely to deliver a realised 3% annualised return in a 2026 sale.** A 3% gross or paper gain is arithmetically possible for a low-entry or superior unit, but it is not the representative outcome supported by current transactions. For an August 2025 buyer selling around September 2026, the applicable 12% Seller's Stamp Duty overwhelms the modest price movement observed so far.

The most useful case is the **990 sqft 3BR Premium**. Its August 2025 median entry was **S$1.993 million / S$2,013 psf**. A September 2026 sale needs approximately **S$2.058 million / S$2,079 psf** merely to show 3% gross annualised price growth, and approximately **S$2.497 million / S$2,522 psf** to retain 3% after the original BSD, a S$5,000 acquisition allowance, 2.5% selling friction and 12% SSD. Mortgage interest, ABSD, property tax and other holding costs are still excluded.

The working 2026 owner-exit range for an ordinary 990 sqft unit is **S$1.95 million–S$2.05 million**. A demonstrably superior high-floor, stack or facing may reach **S$2.06 million–S$2.10 million**, but that is an upper case. At the S$2.058 million gross-growth threshold, the cost model still produces an approximately **S$308,000 loss** before financing and holding costs.

There is no achieved owner exit on which to anchor a stronger conclusion. The repository's URA-source ledger through July 2026 contains **340 New Sales and zero Sub Sales or Resales** at the project. Eighteen 990 sqft developer sales in 2026 had a median of only **S$2.008 million / S$2,028.5 psf**, about **0.78% above** the August 2025 median quantum. Different floors and stacks are not paired repeat sales.

## Three-percent annualised-return test

The gross-price test asks only what sale price makes the apartment price compound by 3% for approximately 13 months:

`gross target = purchase price × 1.03^(13/12)`

The cost-adjusted test compounds the acquisition basis and requires sale proceeds after SSD and selling friction to equal that future value:

`cost-adjusted target = (purchase price + original BSD + S$5,000) × 1.03^(13/12) ÷ (1 − 2.5% − 12%)`

| 797 sqft | S$1.5820m / S$48,700 | **S$1.633m / S$2,050 psf** | **S$1.975m / S$2,478 psf** | about **-S$239k** |
| 872 sqft | S$1.7324m / S$56,220 | **S$1.789m / S$2,051 psf** | **S$2.166m / S$2,484 psf** | about **-S$264k** |
| 883 sqft | S$1.7382m / S$56,510 | **S$1.795m / S$2,033 psf** | **S$2.173m / S$2,461 psf** | about **-S$265k** |
| **990 sqft** | **S$1.9930m / S$69,250** | **S$2.058m / S$2,079 psf** | **S$2.497m / S$2,522 psf** | about **-S$308k** |

The gross thresholds are close enough to current developer evidence that a low-entry or physically superior unit could display 3% paper growth. The cost-adjusted thresholds are about S$2,461–S$2,522 psf, materially above the project's captured record of approximately S$2,151 psf. There is no transaction evidence for that 2026 outcome.

These are capital-return calculations, not leveraged equity returns. Mortgage principal repayment is not a profit or loss; interest and other unrecovered ownership costs are losses. An owner-specific internal rate of return also needs the timing and amount of every progressive payment, tax, fee and sale receipt.

## SSD timing is decisive

The [IRAS residential SSD schedule](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-property/selling-or-disposing-property/seller%27s-stamp-duty-%28ssd%29-for-residential-property) applies the revised four-year period to homes acquired on or after 4 July 2025:

| Holding period | SSD rate on the higher of sale price or market value |
| --- | ---: |
| Up to 1 year | 16% |
| More than 1 year and up to 2 years | **12%** |
| More than 2 years and up to 3 years | 8% |
| More than 3 years and up to 4 years | 4% |
| After the four-year period | 0% |

IRAS generally determines the acquisition and disposal dates from the accepted Option to Purchase or Sale and Purchase Agreement, not the expected completion date. A September 2026 disposal by an August 2025 buyer generally falls in the 12% tier. If the legally effective sale occurs before the exact first anniversary, 16% applies and every result becomes worse. The stamped documents and conveyancing lawyer must determine the operative dates.

## Seller action thresholds

1. **Identify the exact format and entry.** Confirm 797, 872, 883 or 990 sqft, floor, stack, facing, purchase price, incentives and stamped acquisition date.
2. **Obtain written SSD advice.** Do not rely on the public transaction month or an approximate anniversary.
3. **Treat S$1.95m–S$2.05m as the generic 990 sqft test range.** Require strong unit-specific evidence before assuming S$2.06m–S$2.10m.
4. **Price against developer competition.** Ask for a dated developer balance sheet, net prices after incentives and the exact competing stacks—not portal card counts.
5. **Demand achieved owner evidence.** The first exercised Canberra Crescent three-bedroom Sub Sale should outweigh every current advertisement and developer cohort comparison.
6. **Compute actual economic and cash outcomes separately.** Deduct financing, ABSD, property tax and all holding costs for economic profit; separately deduct bank redemption and CPF refund to calculate cash released.
7. **Prefer the SSD-free/completion horizon unless forced to sell.** A 2026 disposal needs an exceptional buyer merely to overcome tax and friction.
