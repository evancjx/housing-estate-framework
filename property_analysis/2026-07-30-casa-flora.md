## Decision

Casa Flora is a **30-unit freehold project completed in 1987**. Its current
public resale market is unusually thin: the dedicated 99.co result displayed
only one apparent physical unit, a 3,122 sqft three-bedroom home asking
S$3.10m, or about S$993 psf.

That low PSF is not automatically cheap. It applies to a very large and
potentially inefficient plan, while the most recent captured caveat is from
September 2024. The closest public large-format achieved anchor is an
approximately 3,120 sqft S$1.90m transaction in September 2020, outside the
repository's latest-60-month capture. The present ask is about S$1.20m, or
63%, above that old quantum. Time, condition and title differences prevent a
matched-repeat-sale conclusion.

The more recent standard-format evidence is stronger but not directly
transferable: 1,561 sqft homes achieved S$2.00m in July 2024 and S$2.06m in
September 2024, or about S$1,281–S$1,320 psf. A large home's lower PSF can be
reasonable because absolute quantum, stairs, voids, outdoor area, renovation
cost and a smaller next-buyer pool suppress its rate.

For the current approximately 3,122 sqft format, the entry discipline is:

| Negotiated purchase price | Assessment |
|---:|---|
| At or below S$2.70m | Potentially compelling if title, condition and area allocation pass review |
| S$2.70m–S$2.85m | Defensible for a strong own-stay unit with controlled renovation scope |
| S$2.85m–S$3.00m | Lifestyle-led; needs exceptional condition, layout and holding power |
| S$3.00m–S$3.10m | Thin margin of safety; current scarcity ask is not achieved value |
| Above S$3.10m | Not supported by evidence captured here |

At an illustrative negotiated price of S$2.85m, acquisition basis before
financing and renovation is about **S$2.9671m**. A S$500,000 capital-only
profit then requires an exit around **S$3.556m**, equivalent to roughly 4.53%
annual sale-price growth over five years or 2.81% over eight.

The conclusion is **moderate conditional absolute-dollar optionality, not
high-confidence huge risk-adjusted quantum**. The project offers rare freehold
space and a plausible future Bedok South MRT access benefit. Those positives
are offset by one-seller price discovery, old-building expenditure, an
illiquid giant format and no current matched large-unit caveat.

## Scope and evidence rules

This is a property analysis, not a factor audit, formal valuation or
cross-tenure ranking. It separates:

- **public sale advertisements** from achieved transactions;
- **large-format and standard-format homes** rather than pooling their PSF;
- **captured URA-derived caveats** from exact-title due diligence; and
- **capital-only scenarios** from forecasts or net investment returns.

describes the broader source basis.

## Valuation and negotiation

### Standard-format evidence

The two 2024 caveats show that ordinary approximately 1,561 sqft homes moved
to S$1,281–S$1,320 psf. Applied mechanically to 3,122 sqft, that range would
produce an implausibly high S$4.00m–S$4.12m quantum. Large homes typically
receive a lower PSF because the buyer pool and financing burden shrink as
quantum rises. The exercise demonstrates why a single project-wide PSF is
unsafe.

### Large-format evidence

At S$3.10m, the card's S$993 psf sits below recent smaller-unit PSF but far
above the old S$609 psf large-format reference. The ask can be defensible only
if current evidence supports a substantial bridge, including:

- superior renovation and maintenance condition;
- efficient enclosed floor area;
- strong privacy, light, ventilation and aspect;
- no major near-term management-corporation works; and
- a realistic next-buyer pool at S$3m-plus.

The indicative offer bands in the Decision are analytical discipline, not a
valuation. A professional valuer and lender may treat non-standard area,
alterations or comparables differently.

### No collective-sale premium

No en-bloc premium is included. Freehold tenure, 30 homes and a 1987
completion do not prove requisite owner consent, planning yield, developer
appetite, a successful tender, timing or net proceeds. Treat any collective
sale as a zero-value option until supported by documented process.

## Capital-only model

This common model uses a **S$2.85m negotiated price**, not the S$3.10m ask.
Its **SC first-property/no-ABSD baseline** assumes a Singapore citizen buying
a first residential property.

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Purchase price | S$2,850,000 |
| Buyer's Stamp Duty | S$112,100 |
| Acquisition legal / technical allowance | S$5,000 |
| **Friction-only acquisition basis** | **S$2,967,100** |
| Illustrative exit friction | 2.5% of sale price |
| **Friction-only breakeven exit** | **S$3,043,179** |
| **Exit for S$500,000 capital-only profit** | **S$3,556,000** |

BSD follows
[IRAS's residential BSD tiers](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-property/buying-or-acquiring-property/buyer%27s-stamp-duty-%28bsd%29).
The scenario assumes dutiable value equals the S$2.85m entry price. Actual BSD
and ABSD are charged on the **higher of consideration or market value**, so an
apparently discounted purchase can attract duty on a higher value.

The basis excludes renovation, financing, maintenance, property tax,
insurance and opportunity cost. A giant 1987 home could require a material
separate technical and renovation reserve.

The equations are:

`basis = purchase + BSD + S$5,000`

`capital-only profit = 0.975 × exit price − basis`

`breakeven exit = basis ÷ 0.975`

`target exit = (basis + S$500,000) ÷ 0.975`

The exact target is about S$3.556m; the rounded display does not change the
underlying calculation.

### ABSD and SSD

Under
[current IRAS ABSD rules](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-property/buying-or-acquiring-property/additional-buyer%27s-stamp-duty-%28absd%29),
a Singapore citizen buying a first residential property has no ABSD, while a
second attracts 20%. At S$2.85m, 20% ABSD is S$570,000 and raises
friction-only breakeven to about **S$3.628m** before finance and ownership
cost. Buyer profile can destroy the thesis.

For residential property bought on or after 4 July 2025,
[current IRAS SSD rules](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-property/selling-or-disposing-property/seller%27s-stamp-duty-%28ssd%29-for-residential-property)
apply 16%, 12%, 8% and 4% rates in the first four holding years respectively.
No SSD applies after the exact fourth anniversary under current rules.
Confirm dates and rates at transaction time.

## Due diligence before an offer

1. Pull a fresh URA transaction search and professional valuation; reconcile
   every recent Casa Flora caveat with exact size and date.
2. Confirm the live card's subsidiary lot, seller authority, asking history,
   occupation status and whether another advertisement describes the same
   home.
3. Obtain the title, strata plan, approved floor plans and alteration records;
   separate enclosed area from void, stairs, balcony, terrace and
   common-property elements.
4. Test whether the September 2020 large-format transaction is the same or a
   genuinely comparable title; do not infer from rounded area alone.
5. Commission building, waterproofing, electrical, plumbing,
   air-conditioning and pest inspections appropriate to a large 1987 home.
6. Price renovation and a 15%–20% contingency before setting the maximum
   offer.
7. Read at least three years of MCST accounts, AGM/EGM minutes, insurance,
   sinking-fund records, by-laws, disputes and planned major works.
8. Obtain two lender indications and ask how non-standard area or alterations
   affect valuation and loan quantum.
9. Obtain exact-unit rental appraisals and build a net-yield budget rather
   than scaling small-unit rents.
10. Walk Casa Flora to Tanah Merah MRT and the future Bedok South station site;
    check traffic, aircraft, construction and night-time conditions.
11. Model Bedok Rise and Bayshore competition under slower sale and rental
    assumptions.
12. Confirm citizenship, property count, BSD, ABSD and SSD position with a
    conveyancing lawyer before exercising an option.

## Bottom line

Casa Flora's present opportunity is rare freehold space, not a proven
high-yield or repeat-sale arbitrage. The single S$3.10m card offers a visibly
low S$993 psf headline, but recent achieved evidence is for half-sized homes
and the closest giant-format anchor is nearly six years old.

A negotiated price around **S$2.70m–S$2.85m** can create conditional
absolute-dollar optionality for a buyer who values the space, can fund
renovation and can wait for a similarly unusual next buyer. Near S$3.10m, the
seller captures most of the scarcity story upfront. Do not call the potential
“huge” until title, usable area, condition, exact comparables and exit
liquidity all survive due diligence.
