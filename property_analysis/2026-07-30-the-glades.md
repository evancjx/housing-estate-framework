## Decision

The Glades offers a useful combination of low entry quantum, Tanah Merah MRT
access, active rental demand and unusually healthy resale turnover. It also
trades below younger Grandeur Park on comparable size cohorts. This creates
**selective value**, but not a proven project-wide valuation gap and not
high-confidence huge appreciation from current asking prices.

The best conventional investment cohort is **667–721 sqft two-bedroom stock**.
The latest trailing evidence spans S$1.02m–S$1.35m with a S$1.209m median.
Recent ordinary 689 sqft resales achieved S$1.19m–S$1.20m, while the latest
721 sqft resale reached S$1.345m in July 2026. An evidence-backed buy box is
therefore approximately **S$1.17m–S$1.24m for 667–689 sqft** and
**S$1.25m–S$1.35m for a genuinely superior 721 sqft unit**. Area, floor, stack
and format matter more than a blended project PSF.

The captured 688 sqft advertisement at S$1.265m, or about S$1,839 psf, is
roughly 5%–6% above the two April 2026 689 sqft caveats. It is negotiable
rather than obviously cheap. The July 721 sqft high does not automatically
justify the same PSF on a smaller or inferior unit.

Other buy-box reads are:

1. **Lowest quantum:** conventional 474–506 sqft stock near
   S$800k–S$835k. The advertised S$850k 474 sqft unit is modestly ahead of
   recent S$820k caveats, not a deep discount.
2. **Compact family format:** 840 sqft around S$1.44m–S$1.55m, with the upper
   end reserved for high floor, good facing and condition.
3. **990–1,066 sqft family stock:** approximately S$1.70m–S$1.85m on matching
   evidence. A S$2.10m/1,066 sqft ask is aggressive without an exceptional
   unit.
4. **Dual-key 1,378 sqft:** the S$2.68m ask is a special-use purchase with no
   close trailing caveat. The advertised combined rent is useful only after
   both tenancies are verified; one stated lease had already reached its
   expiry by the research date.
5. **Do not mark to anomalies:** a June 2026 764 sqft resale at S$980k and
   S$1,282 psf is far below neighbouring evidence. It may reflect a
   ground-floor, condition, related-party or other unit-specific issue and is
   not a project-wide valuation anchor.

At a representative S$1.22m purchase of a conventional 689 sqft two-bedroom,
a capital-only S$500,000 gain requires an exit around **S$1.803m**, or about
**S$2,618 psf**. That requires approximately 8.13% annual appreciation over
five years or 5.01% over eight years before financing and holding costs.
Accordingly, huge absolute quantum is a bull case; lower entry quantum helps
liquidity but makes a very large dollar gain mathematically harder.

## Project proposition

The Glades is a 99-year leasehold development whose lease commenced in 2013,
leaving approximately **85–86 years** at the research date. It comprises
**726 residential homes plus three shops**; portal totals that include the
shops should not be presented as the residential unit count. Construction was
completed in 2016, while public portals variously record TOP in early or late
2017. Lum Chang's project record describes the development and construction
completion:
[official project page](https://www.lumchang.com.sg/projects_uri/the-glades/).

A representative straight-line OneMap coordinate measurement is approximately
**287 metres to Tanah Merah MRT**. This is not a gate-to-platform promise.
Tanah Merah serves the East-West Line and the Changi Airport branch, supporting
both owner-occupier convenience and airport/business-park tenant demand.

The project is established resale stock rather than an integrated development.
Its three shops are not equivalent to Sceneca's station-linked retail
proposition. The relative discount to newer, closer projects must therefore be
treated as partly structural, not free convergence upside.

## Five- and eight-year capital-only test

The illustration uses an **S$1.22m purchase of a conventional 689 sqft
two-bedroom** by a Singapore citizen buying a first residential property.
It assumes dutiable value equals the entry price; actual BSD and ABSD apply to
the higher of purchase consideration or market value.

Assumptions:

- residential BSD under current marginal tiers: **S$33,400**;
- acquisition legal/due-diligence allowance: **S$5,000**;
- day-one capital basis: **S$1,258,400**;
- net exit proceeds: **97.5% of exit price**, allowing 2.5% sale friction;
- no ABSD and sale after the applicable SSD period; and
- no financing, maintenance, tax, renovation, rent or opportunity cost.

Formula:

`capital profit = 0.975 × S$1.22m × (1 + annual growth)^years − S$1.2584m`.

| Annual price growth | Five-year exit | Five-year capital profit/loss | Eight-year exit | Eight-year capital profit/loss |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0% | S$1.220m | -S$69k | S$1.220m | -S$69k |
| 2% | S$1.347m | +S$55k | S$1.429m | +S$135k |
| 3% | S$1.414m | +S$121k | S$1.546m | +S$248k |
| 4% | S$1.484m | +S$189k | S$1.670m | +S$370k |
| 5% | S$1.557m | +S$260k | S$1.803m | +S$499k |

Capital break-even is approximately **S$1.291m**. Large absolute-profit
thresholds are:

| Profit target | Required exit | Approx. PSF on 689 sqft | Five-year annual growth | Eight-year annual growth |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Break-even | S$1.291m | S$1,873 | 1.13% | 0.71% |
| S$500k | S$1.803m | S$2,618 | 8.13% | 5.01% |
| S$1.0m | S$2.316m | S$3,362 | 13.68% | 8.34% |

For a Singapore citizen buying a **second** residential property, 20% ABSD
adds S$244,000. Simplified capital break-even rises to **S$1.541m**, or about
S$2,236 psf. That requires approximately 4.78% annual growth over five years
or 2.96% over eight years merely to recover entry and exit friction before
holding costs.

Current references:
[IRAS BSD](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-property/buying-or-acquiring-property/buyer%27s-stamp-duty-%28bsd%29),
[IRAS ABSD](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-property/buying-or-acquiring-property/additional-buyer%27s-stamp-duty-%28absd%29)
and
[IRAS SSD](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-property/selling-or-disposing-property/seller%27s-stamp-duty-%28ssd%29-for-residential-property).
For purchases from 4 July 2025, current SSD generally applies at 16%, 12%, 8%
and 4% within the first, second, third and fourth year respectively. The exact
four-year anniversary, buyer profile and law at disposal must be checked.

## Risks and limits to upside

- **Older than the newest benchmark:** The Glades cannot simply converge to
  Grandeur or Sceneca PSF.
- **Large internal and external substitute pool:** 726 homes plus several
  neighbouring projects can constrain exit pricing.
- **Outlier caveats:** the S$980k/764 sqft sale and other low-PSF results
  require investigation, not blind exclusion or blind adoption.
- **Compact-layout competition:** many 667–721 sqft units can compete for the
  same investor/tenant pool.
- **Dual-key complexity:** split use, privacy, utilities, tenancy legality,
  valuation and future buyer preferences can narrow the exit market.
- **Finite tenure:** roughly 85–86 years is adequate for this hold but provides
  no freehold scarcity premium.
- **Rail, road, depot and aircraft environment:** noise and view effects vary
  sharply by block, floor and facing.
- **Construction pipeline:** future GLS work can cause temporary noise/dust
  and later add competing stock.
- **MCST/ageing risk:** waterproofing, façades, lifts, pools, mechanical
  systems and sinking-fund adequacy need review as the project matures.
- **Policy and carrying costs:** ABSD can destroy the second-property case;
  SSD makes a short resale strategy expensive.

## Due diligence before an option to purchase

1. Obtain exact block, unit, floor, stack, registered area, floor plan and a
   written seller-authorised executable price.
2. Prove same-day availability and deduplicate advertisements using photos,
   descriptions, facing, tenancy and agent authority.
3. Pull exact-size and same-floor-band caveats, investigate low-price
   anomalies and obtain a bank valuation.
4. Inspect useful internal area versus balcony/void/patio, privacy, west sun,
   rail/road/depot/aircraft noise, view and future construction exposure.
5. Review complete tenancy documents, stamp certificates, deposits, payment
   records, renewal/expiry, break clauses, inventory and repair obligations.
6. For dual-key stock, verify approved configuration, separate access, fire
   safety, utilities, legal occupancy and bank-valuation treatment.
7. Review MCST accounts, maintenance/sinking charges, AGM minutes, defects,
   insurance, special levies and major contracts.
8. Verify title, encumbrances, seller acquisition date/SSD and buyer-specific
   BSD, ABSD/remission, TDSR, CPF/cash and financing stress.

## Final assessment

The Glades has **good liquidity, useful rental fallback and moderate
appreciation potential**. Its strongest value lies in buying a conventional
667–721 sqft two-bedroom at or below exact-layout evidence, not in treating the
entire project as a cheap Tanah Merah proxy.

The evidence does not support high-confidence huge quantum potential. A
five-year S$500,000 capital-only gain from a S$1.22m entry needs roughly
S$2,618 psf and 8.13% annual price growth. Even over eight years, about 5.01%
annual growth is required before holding costs. The low ticket supports
sellability, but the same low ticket makes a huge absolute dollar profit hard.

Buy only after resolving outlier caveats, exact strata utility, noise/facing,
tenancy and future-supply exposure. At a verified discount to matching caveats,
The Glades can be a sensible defensive hold; at premium asks or with 20% ABSD,
expect weak risk-adjusted capital economics.

project plans, tax rules and market conditions can change after capture and
must be refreshed immediately before an offer.
