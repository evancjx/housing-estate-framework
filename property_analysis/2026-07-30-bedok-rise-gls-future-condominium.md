The award equals approximately **S$1,330 per square foot per plot ratio**
before construction, finance, professional fees, marketing, common facilities,
developer margin and taxes. That land rate does not mechanically determine a
launch price.

Because no units exist for sale, the actionable conclusion is a
**prospective price-discipline framework**, not a buy recommendation:

| Hypothetical average / selected-unit launch rate | Prospective assessment |
|---:|---|
| At or below S$2,400 psf | Potentially attractive if specifications, facing and maintenance are sound |
| S$2,400–S$2,550 psf | Defensible for strong layouts and genuine Bedok South access |
| S$2,550–S$2,700 psf | Requires superior product, stack and scarcity evidence |
| Above S$2,700 psf | Thin margin of safety against captured nearby resale context |

These are analytical thresholds, not developer guidance or a valuation.
At an illustrative 900 sqft unit and S$2,500 psf, the purchase quantum would
be S$2.25m. Its friction-only acquisition basis is about **S$2.3371m**, and a
S$500,000 capital-only profit requires an exit around **S$2.910m**. That
requires approximately 5.28% annual sale-price growth over five years or
3.27% over eight, before financing and ownership cost.

The potential is therefore **moderate and entry-price dependent, not proven
huge quantum**. A new home near future Bedok South MRT may receive a new-build
and access premium. But the buyer will face developer pricing, construction
and completion risk, future supply at Bayshore, and a later resale market that
must absorb a much higher absolute quantum than nearby older stock.

## Land basis

The approximate land rate is calculated from official award inputs:

`land rate = S$464.8m ÷ (32,470 sqm × 10.7639 sqft per sqm)`

`land rate ≈ S$1,330 psf ppr`

This is an analytical conversion. It is not the developer's accounting cost
and excludes:

- construction and fit-out;
- professional, authority and infrastructure charges;
- finance and holding cost;
- marketing, commissions and show-flat cost;
- landscaping, facilities and common areas;
- development risk, taxes and margin; and
- changes in cost or policy before launch.

A launch premium over land rate is normal. What matters to a buyer is whether
the all-in unit quantum remains supportable by own-stay utility, competing new
launches and eventual resale evidence.

## Capital-only illustration

This common framework is deliberately hypothetical:

- illustrative strata area: **900 sqft**;
- illustrative purchase rate: **S$2,500 psf**; and
- illustrative purchase price: **S$2.25m**.

It is not inventory, a forecast, an official indication or a valuation.

The **SC first-property/no-ABSD baseline** assumes a Singapore citizen buying
a first residential property.

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Hypothetical purchase price | S$2,250,000 |
| Buyer's Stamp Duty | S$82,100 |
| Acquisition legal / technical allowance | S$5,000 |
| **Friction-only acquisition basis** | **S$2,337,100** |
| Illustrative exit friction | 2.5% of sale price |
| **Friction-only breakeven exit** | **S$2,397,026** |
| **Exit for S$500,000 capital-only profit** | **S$2,909,846** |

BSD follows
[IRAS's residential BSD tiers](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-property/buying-or-acquiring-property/buyer%27s-stamp-duty-%28bsd%29).
The scenario assumes dutiable value equals the hypothetical S$2.25m entry
price. Actual BSD and ABSD are charged on the **higher of consideration or market
value**, so an apparently discounted purchase can attract duty on a higher
value.

The basis excludes progressive-payment finance, maintenance after completion,
property tax, insurance, furnishing, defects, opportunity cost and any
extension or delay.

The equations are:

`basis = purchase + BSD + S$5,000`

`capital-only profit = 0.975 × exit price − basis`

`breakeven exit = basis ÷ 0.975`

`target exit = (basis + S$500,000) ÷ 0.975`

|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 years | 1% | S$2,364,773 | **−S$31,447** |
| 5 years | 3% | S$2,608,367 | **S$206,058** |
| 5 years | 5% | S$2,871,634 | **S$462,743** |
| 8 years | 1% | S$2,436,428 | **S$38,417** |

The S$500,000 target requires approximately:

- **5.28% annual sale-price growth over five years**; or
- **3.27% annual sale-price growth over eight years**.

Every result is before finance, progressive-payment cost, ownership expense,
tax, furnishing and opportunity cost. The target is capital-only, not a net
return.

### ABSD and SSD

Under
[current IRAS ABSD rules](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-property/buying-or-acquiring-property/additional-buyer%27s-stamp-duty-%28absd%29),
a Singapore citizen buying a first residential property has no ABSD, while a
second attracts 20%. At the hypothetical S$2.25m price, 20% ABSD is S$450,000
and raises friction-only breakeven to about **S$2.859m** before finance and
ownership cost.

For residential property bought on or after 4 July 2025,
[current IRAS SSD rules](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-property/selling-or-disposing-property/seller%27s-stamp-duty-%28ssd%29-for-residential-property)
apply 16%, 12%, 8% and 4% rates in the first four holding years respectively.
No SSD applies after the exact fourth anniversary under current rules. For a
building-under-construction purchase, contract date and completion date are
different; obtain conveyancing advice before planning an exit.

## Due diligence once launch documents exist

1. Confirm the official project name, developer entity, sale licence, tenure
   commencement, expected TOP and final approved unit count.
2. Obtain the approved site plan, floor plans, strata-area schedule and
   schedule of finishes; reject agent-redrawn plans as legal evidence.
3. Recalculate every selected stack's quantum, BSD, ABSD, progressive-payment
   interest and required exit price.
4. Compare exact-size achieved new-sale, sub-sale and resale transactions at
   Sceneca and other matched projects using a fresh URA search.
5. Recheck all current and confirmed GLS/private supply, especially Bayshore
   and New Upper Changi Road, against the expected launch and completion
   windows.
6. Verify Bedok South MRT operational status and walk from the actual proposed
   residential gate to the station, retail, schools and parks.
7. Inspect the physical site at morning, evening and weekend periods for road,
   rail, aircraft, construction, privacy and neighbouring-development
   exposure.
8. Compare low-, middle- and high-floor stacks for blocked-view and future
   development risk rather than paying a generic project average.
9. Obtain at least two lender IPAs and test valuation support at the chosen
   PSF and absolute quantum.
10. Build a post-completion net-rental model from matched area bands; include
    maintenance, vacancy, tax, agents, repairs and furnishing.
11. Read the Option to Purchase and S&P with a conveyancing lawyer, including
    progressive payment, permitted changes, defects and assignment / SSD
    implications.
12. Confirm citizenship, property count, BSD, ABSD and SSD position before
    booking or exercising any option.

## Bottom line

Bedok Rise is a credible future-project watchlist candidate, not an available
condominium investment. The awarded approximately S$1,330 psf-ppr land basis,
future Bedok South station relationship and east-side new-build scarcity can
support buyer interest. They do not prove that a launch price will leave room
for resale profit.

The project begins to look attractive at **S$2,400 psf or below**, remains
selectively defensible around **S$2,400–S$2,550 psf**, and needs exceptional
product evidence above that. A future buyer should judge the exact stack and
absolute quantum, not celebrate a project-wide headline. Until licensed
inventory, final plans and a price list exist, any claim of “huge potential
quantum” is speculation.
