## 2. Sequencing Pattern Analysis

### The only MRT-first estate

| Town | MRT Lag | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Sembawang (NS11 corridor) | −1 yr | Sembawang MRT Feb 1996, first HDB 1997 — effectively concurrent. The Canberra subzone within the same town has a +21-year housing-first lag. |

Only one of 23 towns is technically MRT-first by one year. All others are housing-first. This means **housing-first is the norm**, not the exception — the framework should not treat it as a binary flag but as a continuous lag.

### MRT lag distribution

| Band | Towns |
|---|---|
| 1–6 yrs (near-concurrent) | Pasir Ris (1), Bishan (2), Punggol (2), Sengkang (6), Yishun (6), Tampines (6) |
| 7–14 yrs (moderate gap) | Bukit Batok (7), Jurong East (10), Clementi (11), Bukit Panjang (13), CCK (13), Bedok (14) |
| 15–28 yrs (severe) | Serangoon (19), Woodlands (24), Jurong West (25), Bukit Merah (27), Geylang (28), Hougang (28), Kallang/Whampoa (28), Queenstown (28) |
| 38–50 yrs (extreme) | Bukit Timah (38), Marine Parade (50) |

### Chronological explanation for lags

Towns announced **before 1975** have lags exceeding 20 years because the MRT network didn't exist yet — this is not a policy failure. Towns announced **1979–1988** have 6–14 year lags (housing built as EWL/NSL was being planned). Towns announced **from 1994 onward** have lags under 6 years — the NEL, Sengkang LRT, and Punggol LRT were designed concurrently with the towns. For post-1994 towns, any gap above 6 years is a genuine planning lag.

## 5. Schools — Fastest Amenity, Weak Differentiator

Primary schools are the fastest amenity to arrive in new towns: most within 0–3 years of first HDB occupation. Exceptions:

- Hougang: 9 years (1975 → 1984) — longest greenfield lag
- Woodlands: 6 years (1972 → 1978)
- Bukit Batok: 3 years

Negative lags (school predating HDB town) are common in pre-existing urban areas: Geylang (−38), Serangoon (−24), CCK (−18), Kallang/Whampoa (−11), Bukit Merah (−6), Queenstown (−4). These are not supply failures — established schools served incoming HDB residents.

School provision is **not a useful discriminating variable** for sequencing quality. It should be scored on current facility count and quality, not on historical lag.

## 6. SERS Distribution

SERS activity concentrates in towns with 1961–1978 HDB stock:

| High SERS towns | Count | Stock vintage |
|---|---|---|
| Bukit Merah | 13 | 1961-era + SIT inheritance |
| Clementi | 7 | 1977–1985 |
| Kallang/Whampoa | 5 | 1961–1972 |
| Queenstown | 3+ | 1960–1970s |
| Bedok / Jurong East / Jurong West | 2 each | 1975–1980s |

Zero-SERS towns fall into two categories: young stock (Sengkang, Punggol, Sembawang, Bishan, Bukit Batok, Pasir Ris — mostly post-1983) or height-constrained (Hougang, Tampines — Paya Lebar Airbase 20-floor cap prevents redensification that makes SERS financially viable for HDB). The Paya Lebar Airbase is scheduled to relocate ~2030, which may unlock future SERS in Hougang and Tampines.

SERS is a **lagging indicator of stock vintage**, not a current provision signal. Count positively only if the SERS replacement adds better-provisioned flats; don't treat SERS history as a quality marker in itself.

## 7. Key Framework Implications

### S8 (Infrastructure Readiness) — calibration corrections

**MRT lag bands should map to distinct S8 penalties:**

| MRT Lag | S8 Implication |
|---|---|
| 0–6 years or MRT-first | Full infrastructure credit |
| 7–14 years | Moderate penalty: −0.3 to −0.5 |
| 15–24 years | Significant penalty: −0.5 to −0.8 |
| 25+ years | Structural gap flag: −0.8 to −1.0 |

Housing-first is universal — don't treat it as binary. The meaningful axis is the magnitude.

**Polyclinic gap should carry more weight in S8 than MRT lag for estates built after 1980**, because MRT lags are largely network-availability artefacts while polyclinic lags reflect direct policy choice.

### S6 (Healthcare) — do not use HDB classification as proxy

Mature estates with late or missing polyclinics (Bishan ~45-year wait, Kallang/Whampoa 60-year wait, Serangoon 41-year wait) would score incorrectly if "mature estate → good healthcare provision" is assumed. Score on verified current polyclinic count and distance, not on estate age.

### G archetype (early-stage new town) — post-1994 standard

For towns announced post-1994 (Punggol, Sengkang, Sembawang, Tengah), concurrent transport planning is the baseline expectation. MRT lag above 6 years in a post-1994 town is a genuine planning deficiency, not a network-availability lag.

### Amenity-gap towns — framework flags to add

The following towns warrant explicit D-multiplier scrutiny for missing amenities that are materially overdue relative to town age:

| Town | Flag |
|---|---|
| Bishan | No hawker centre, no polyclinic as of 2026; both planned ~2030 |
| CCK | No standalone hawker centre as of 2026; planned ~2027 |
| Kallang/Whampoa | Polyclinic only arrived 2021 after 60-year wait — now mitigated |
| Serangoon | Polyclinic opened Nov 2025 — was a 41-year gap |
| Jurong West | Pioneer Polyclinic only opened 2017 (54-year gap from 1963 JTC flats) |
| Marine Parade | MRT only arrived June 2024 (50-year gap) — now mitigated; monitor TEL station integration |
