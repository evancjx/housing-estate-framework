Three useful integration tests

Each test isolates a different lived-experience proposition. There is no universal premium and no unified ranking.

Town-centre test

North Park

The strongest full-integration control: retail and transport-hub proposition, but with a 2015 lease and Yishun town-centre positioning.

Retail podium test

Wisteria & Nine

Tests daily retail at the project without assuming interchange-level access. Tenant mix, strata governance and actual walking routes require buyer diligence.

Near-rail test

Canberra peers

Watergardens, Commodore and Canberra Residences help distinguish simple station proximity from a mixed-use or integrated development proposition.

Buyer-fit matrix

Choose the proposition first, then inspect bedroom-matched achieved prices. Asking listings and projected rents are outside this evidence set.

Frequent rail user

Validate route, not radius

Straight-line distance is a screening diagnostic. Entrances, crossings, shelter, gradients and actual walking time are not represented.

Convenience-first household

Audit retail depth

Distinguish a neighbourhood podium from a large town-centre mall and bus interchange. “Mixed use” does not guarantee the same service depth.

Value-focused buyer

Price space and lease

Compare quantum, PSF, median area and lease start together. A lower PSF can reflect more area, older lease or weaker integration—not automatic value.

Exit-focused buyer

Use actual exit states

Give more weight to Sub Sale/Resale depth than developer New Sale absorption when testing future liquidity, while preserving location and vintage caveats.

Spatial boundary

Distances are project-coordinate to station-coordinate haversine calculations. They are not walking routes, and retail labels are qualitative factual profiles—not price adjustments or guaranteed liveability outcomes.
